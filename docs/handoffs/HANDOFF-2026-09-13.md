## 2. The finding that drove F and G, and it is the useful one

Five rig walks were dominated by RIGHT (69-107 turns of ~120 actions).
On the walk where **both tiers saw the basket** it still did not converge.

So the blocker was neither arbitration nor recognition:

- `_local_scene` emitted `SCAN_ACTION` on every free frame, and at
  `cold_search_after` 6 that is five frames in six. **The stand-in
  outvoted the cloud 5:1.** (Phase F)
- On the 37 frames where perception said DETECTED, the action was RIGHT
  28 / FORWARD 4. `_direction_for()` computed a bearing, wrote it to
  `_navigate.target_direction`, and it never reached `safest_direction`.
  **Detection had no effect on motion.** (Phase G)

`tools/steer_check.py` replays a walk and reports actions **conditioned
on perception status** — aggregate counts cannot show this, because most
frames detect nothing and swamp the rest. On `...231358`, 33 detected
frames:

    before   RIGHT x33                      bearing matched 16/33 (48%)
    after    RIGHT 18, FORWARD 11, LEFT 4   bearing matched 31/33 (94%)

**The 48% was luck** — RIGHT is right about half the time by chance.

## 8. The rule the metrics pipeline exists to protect

**A percentile belongs to the run that produced it.** Averaging p90s
across runs does not give the p90 of the pair. `control/metrics_routes.py`
returns rows whole and never offers a blended figure (a test asserts the
absence); the dashboard's cross-run numbers are a **median of per-run
values** and say so on screen. `n` is printed beside every percentile,
because a p99 over 8 samples is one sample with a fancy name.
