# 3.84 A person's Stop is the last word over autonomy

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.84; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** Two follow-ups from 3.82
([3.82-refused-goal-hold.md](3.82-refused-goal-hold.md), review round 7
findings 5 and 9;
[handoff](../../handoffs/HANDOFF-2026-10-15-3.82-refused-goal-hold.md)):

1. **A D-pad Stop during explore does not stop the robot for long** (found
   by code reading in 3.82). The D-pad's STOP (`web-twin/app.js`
   `sendAction`) calls only the robot server's `/stop`; the brain's mission
   keeps running (only the brain panel's Stop calls `/mission/stop`). The
   robot server ends nav2's goal and holds `ros` wheel commands at zero;
   explore sees its goal end and posts its next one, naming no one (ranked
   `ros`). The bridge takes it, the route bumps `goal_stop["gen"]` and
   lifts the hold (`world_goal_set`), and the robot drives again within
   seconds of a person's Stop. 3.82's handoff and plan say "the twin's Stop
   also ends the brain's mission": true of the brain panel's Stop only.
2. **A verb clears the Stop's hold without its lock** (safety path).
   `_carry_out`'s `drive: ros` branch sets `goal_stop["hold"] = False` bare;
   every other writer takes `goal_gen_lock`. A Stop that sets the hold while
   a verb arbitrated before it clears it loses the hold, and nav2's `ros`
   wheel commands pass while the Stop's loop is still cancelling.

**Decided with the user (2026-10-15): a person's Stop is the last word over
autonomy.** After it, the brain's goals and verbs are refused by name and
the runner ends the mission `cancelled`, `cancelled_by: "stop"` (3.70 E's
end state). Explore names itself on its goal posts so the server can tell
it from a script. A person resumes with a goal, a D-pad move or a new
mission. Unnamed script goals keep today's behaviour.

**Definitions (written before building).**

* **A person's Stop** is a `POST /stop` whose driver ranks as a person
  (`driver_priority() == DRIVER_MANUAL`): `twin-dpad`, `teleop-operator`,
  an unnamed caller (`unknown`, which M4 ranks as a person on purpose) and
  any name the order does not know. Not a person: `brain` and `teleop`
  (autonomous; their stop already spares the goal, V1) and `ros` (it ends
  the goal as today, but is autonomy, not a person, so it latches nothing).
  `twin-local-brain` (dead, local rank) latches nothing.
* **What it latches.** The robot server records "autonomy stopped" (who,
  when). While it is set, every command from an autonomous driver other
  than `ros` -- `/action`, `/wheels` and a `POST /world/goal` that names
  `brain` or `teleop` -- is refused `reason: "person_stop"`, before
  arbitration. The brain's own `/stop` and `DELETE /world/goal` are never
  refused (stopping is everyone's right). `ros`'s wheel commands keep 3.82's
  rule (held at zero while the Stop's loop ends the goal). Unnamed goals
  rank `ros` (3.23) and keep today's behaviour: accepted, they supersede the
  Stop's loop; they neither set nor clear the latch.
* **When it ends.** Only when a mission STARTS: `POST /mission/start`, after
  its 409 check and BEFORE the build, asks the robot server to lift it
  (`POST /autonomy/resume`), so a D-pad Stop pressed during the build
  latches again (corrected in review round 1: this said "once the runner is
  built"; a start then refused leaves the latch lifted with no mission
  running, which nothing autonomous can use). Not on a person's next verb or goal: those
  always work (a person is never refused by the latch), and the mission the
  Stop interrupted must end, not resume when the person next drives -- a
  person's D-pad move that the brain happens not to collide with would
  otherwise hand the robot back to a mission the person stopped. A person
  "resumes" by driving (their own command works at once) or by starting a
  new mission.
* **The brain's side.** `RemoteRobot` and the runner's navigator gate raise
  `StoppedByPerson` (a `Preempted`) on `person_stop`; the runner ends the
  mission `cancelled`, `cancelled_by: "stop"`, its reason naming the
  person's Stop. Explore sends `x-driver: brain` on its goal posts
  (`RemoteNavigator`).
* **How the twin shows it.** It already words `cancelled_by: "stop"` as
  "Cancelled -- Stop pressed" on the Sim panel's poll; no twin change is
  planned.
* **Bug 2.** `/action` reads `goal_stop["stops"]` as the request arrives
  (as the goal route does since 3.82). Under `drive: ros` the verb lifts the
  hold under `goal_gen_lock`, and only if no stop was counted since. A verb
  that lifts the hold while a Stop's loop is still ending a goal re-raises
  it when the verb ends (if that loop is still the current one): the
  person's twists pass, nav2's goal does not drive after them. Never held
  across a bridge call (3.76 A8).

**Interleavings traced (step A-B design, before review).** P = a person's
Stop, B = the brain's stop, Vp/Vb = a person's / the brain's verb, Gp = a
person's goal, Ge = explore's goal (`brain`), Gs = a script's (unnamed),
R = `/autonomy/resume`, L = the Stop's loop.

| Order | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Ge active, P, L cancels, Ge' | Ge' refused `person_stop` at arrival; the mission ends `cancelled`/`stop`; nav2 has no goal |
| Ge' arrives, P while it waits on `goal_stop_lock` | stale (stops moved) and latched: `person_stop`, never sent |
| Ge' with the bridge, P lands | taken but stops moved: answered `person_stop`; L (newer generation) ends it |
| Vb arbitrated, P, Vb executes | (review round 1) the latch is read again at execution -- under `motion_lock` (direct), in the hold-lift step under `goal_gen_lock` (ROS), and after a direct `/wheels` write (which is zeroed again) -- so Vb is refused `person_stop`; a Stop after that read cuts the move already begun |
| P, Vp | Vp never refused by the latch; under ROS it lifts the hold (stops unchanged since Vp arrived) for its own length; re-held after if L is still ending |
| Vp arbitrated, P, Vp's lift | stops moved: no lift; the hold stays (criterion 5) |
| P, Gp | accepted Gp supersedes L (3.82); the latch stays: the brain remains out |
| P, Gs | as 3.82: accepted, supersedes L; the latch stays |
| B (the brain panel's Stop, `/mission/stop`) | spares the goal (V1); the runner ends its own goal (3.76 C1); no latch |
| P, R (mission start), first Vb | R lifts the latch first; Vb runs |
| R, then P during the build | the latch is set again; the new mission's first command ends it `cancelled`/`stop` |
| P, two Ps | each sets the latch; the newest loop owns the hold |

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **Red first.** Every test written for a bug fails on `dev`
   (`fbb1b0b`) for the reason it names and passes with the fix; the guards
   for behaviour `dev` already has pass on both.
2. **Bug 1, in process** (3.82's fake bridge and fake nav2, nav2 posting
   `ros` wheel commands at 20 Hz while a goal is active; explore over HTTP,
   `_explore_over_http`'s pattern): explore's goal active and driving, a
   D-pad Stop (`x-driver: twin-dpad`; also unnamed and `teleop-operator`).
   Over the next 3 s of ticking: the fastest `ros` wheel speed applied after
   the Stop's first 0.1 s is **0.0 rad/s**, no explore goal reaches the
   bridge, and the mission ends `cancelled` with `cancelled_by: "stop"`
   within **3 ticks** of its first refused command. On `dev` the next goal
   is accepted and nav2 drives (> 0).
3. **Verbs too:** after a person's Stop a `brain` `/action` (and a
   `brain`-named goal) is refused `person_stop`; a mission whose robot is a
   `RemoteRobot` over HTTP ends `cancelled`/`stop` on its next verb.
4. **What stays:** (a) the brain's own `/stop` latches nothing (a `brain`
   verb after it executes); a `ros`-named stop latches nothing; (b) after a
   person's Stop, an unnamed goal is accepted and drives (3.82 criterion 3
   unchanged), a person's verb executes and moves the wheels, a person's
   goal is accepted and drives; (c) after a person's Stop, a new
   `POST /mission/start` lifts the latch and the new mission's first command
   is not refused; (d) a robot server that does not know
   `/autonomy/resume` (404) does not stop a mission starting.
5. **Bug 2, the lost hold:** under `drive: ros` (`tests/test_ros_fallback`
   `Stack`), a person's verb arbitrated before a Stop whose hold clear runs
   after it (forced by a hook between arbitration and the clear): the hold
   is still set after the verb, and nav2's `ros` wheel commands for the old
   goal over the next 0.5 s apply **0.0 rad/s**. On `dev` the hold is lost.
6. **Bug 2, a person's verb during the Stop's loop:** a person's Stop whose
   cancels fail, then a person's verb: the verb executes (its turn > 30 deg
   or its forward move > 0), and after it ends nav2's `ros` commands apply
   **0.0 rad/s** until the loop ends the goal; then the loop lifts the hold
   (the hold is clear within 3 s of the cancels landing).
7. **Live, through the real stack** (robot server, brain, ROS container
   `vision-picar-ros:latest`, `scaled_house`, this section's ports and
   domain 84): one explore mission started through the brain's HTTP API; a
   D-pad Stop (`POST /stop`, `x-driver: twin-dpad`) while its goal is
   active and the robot moving (true speed > 5 cm/s). (a) The robot's TRUE
   displacement (`/world/truth`) from its pose at the Stop's reply over the
   next **10 s** is **<= 5 cm**; (b) the mission ends `cancelled` with
   `cancelled_by: "stop"` within those 10 s; (c) a D-pad FORWARD right after
   executes (true displacement > 0); (d) a new explore mission then starts
   and the robot moves >= 20 cm within 60 s. The same harness on `dev`
   (`fbb1b0b`) is run once for (a) and (b) as the baseline and recorded,
   whatever it shows.
8. **Ground truth for safety:** motion vetting is not changed, so
   `tests/footprint_sweep.py` is not re-run; criterion 7's displacement is
   the truth measurement.
