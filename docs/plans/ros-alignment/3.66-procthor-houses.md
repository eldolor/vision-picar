# 3.66 ProcTHOR floor plans as simulator houses (2026-10-10): criteria, written before building

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.66; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** Every in-process sweep runs in four hand-drawn houses
(`starter_house`, `scaled_house`, `home_first_floor`, `complex_house`). A
policy tuned on four houses can be tuned *to* them, and a bug that needs a
layout none of them has stays hidden. The user asked (2026-10-10) whether
other simulators could be used. The answer we chose: take ProcTHOR-10K's
procedurally generated houses (AI2, Apache-2.0) as **floor plans only** and
rasterise them into our own `GridWorld`, so the sweeps get hundreds of
layouts without a second simulator to disagree with the first (the reason
Gazebo was rejected, `docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`).

## Scope

**In:**
* `tools/procthor_import.py`: download the pinned ProcTHOR-10K `val`
  split and AI2's asset database (both checked by sha256), reduce each
  house to what a 2D grid can use, and write one committed data file.
  The suite never touches the network.
* `sim/maps/procthor.py`: one ProcTHOR house -> `GridWorld`. Selected as
  `SIM_MAP=procthor:val:<index>` through `sim/maps.build_world`, so every
  sweep, the robot server and `/health` take it like any other house.
* A sweep: the `explore` policy over many of these houses, in process.

**Out, and why:**
* **ProcTHOR's images and its renderer (AI2-THOR, Unity).** A third
  picture of the world, and the renders look like a game; perception is
  judged on real photos (Stage 0), not here.
* **Objects on surfaces** (the mug on the table). The grid holds what
  stands on the floor; the lidar and the chassis meet nothing else.
* **Movers** (`SIM_MOVERS`) in these houses. The four hand-drawn houses
  keep them.
* **Live ROS runs (nav2, SLAM) across many of them.** Heavy runs go one at
  a time; one live mission is in criterion 7.

## Design

* **Coordinates.** ProcTHOR's `x` -> grid `x`, its `z` -> grid `y`, plus a
  one-cell margin of wall. (That is a mirror image of AI2-THOR's top view;
  geometry, distances and topology are unchanged.)
* **Walls.** Each room's floor polygon edges are walls, drawn one cell thick
  round the centre line as `home_first_floor` does. A wall ProcTHOR marks
  `empty` is an open-plan boundary and is not drawn. A door's hole
  (`holePolygon`, metres along its wall) is a gap in the wall. The front
  door (`room0 == room1`, one per house) stays shut: the house is closed.
* **Objects.** Top-level objects whose bottom is below 0.25 m (they reach
  the floor band the lidar and the chassis meet), each as its rotated
  bounding-box footprint from the asset database. Tables (dining, side,
  coffee, desk) are their four legs, the repo's convention since
  `home_first_floor`; everything else is solid. A piece smaller than a cell
  takes the cell under its centre. No piece claims a wall cell.
* **Rooms** are the cells whose centres fall in a room polygon, named by
  type (`living room`, `bedroom 2`, ...).
* **The start** is ProcTHOR's agent position, moved to the nearest cell
  whose eight neighbours are free if it is not one.
* **The target**, `red backpack`, one cell, placed by a seed derived from
  the house index: on a free cell the Rover can reach, in a room other
  than the start's, not touching a wall cell or a door gap.
* **A usable house** has three or more rooms, and the Rover (with the
  safety margin, `tests/chassis_fit.py`) starts free and reaches every
  room. The sweep set is the first N usable houses by index: chosen by
  rule, never by hand.

## Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7)

1. **Topology is kept.** Two rooms are joined in the grid when a free floor
   cell of one is 4-adjacent to a free floor cell of the other (objects
   ignored). That graph equals ProcTHOR's (interior doors plus `empty`
   walls) in **>= 95% of the 1,000 `val` houses**. Equal means no door
   lost and no wall leaking.
2. **The outline is kept.** Area of the cells whose centres lie inside the
   union of the room polygons (floor or wall) against the polygons' own
   area: within **+/-5% in >= 95% of houses**.
3. **The furniture is kept.** Of all floor-standing source objects in the
   1,000 houses, **>= 98% are placed** (claim at least one cell).
4. **Most houses are usable** by the rule above: **>= 80% of the `val`
   houses with three or more rooms.** The excluded houses and why are
   recorded.
5. **The sweep runs and finds.** `explore` with `FakeNav`
   (`tests/test_explore.py`'s nav2 stand-in, ground-truth reachability)
   over the **first 100 usable houses**, `max_steps` 200:
   (a) **100%** end with an outcome and no exception within the tick cap;
   (b) **found >= 90%**. Every house not found is classified in this
   entry (importer fault, policy fault, or the target truly hidden).
6. **Offline, pinned and quick.** The suite reads only committed data;
   building one house takes **< 0.5 s median** on the laptop; building
   the same house twice gives an identical grid; the importer refuses a
   source whose sha256 differs from the pinned one. The 100-house sweep
   goes on `SLOW_FILES`.
7. **Through the real path, once.** One `frontier` mission against the
   live local stack (robot server + brain over HTTP) with
   `SIM_MAP=procthor:val:<index>` ends with an outcome, and `/health`
   names that house.

The numbers are pinned in tests as they are measured.
