# 3.103 Explore's goal chooser after the ceiling: lean facts, network shape, then scoring the whole pool

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.103; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-25, after 3.101 found F-rich (42 facts) level
with F-large (17): "Should we look at the architecture of the neural net
instead?", then "Add 1, 2, 3 from above to the plan" -- the three steps
proposed in reply, in order:

1. Let 3.102's oracle ceiling (amendment 1) say whether trips have room.
2. If they do: cut to the facts that matter and try a modest sweep of the
   network's shape.
3. Only then: a chooser that scores the whole pool together.

**What is known going in.** Every learned chooser so far (3.65, 3.96,
3.101) is one network: `score = w . f + MLP(f)`, MLP N -> 32 -> 32 -> 1
(tanh), each candidate scored on its own with the same weights. The shape
was set in 3.65 ("about 32 units") and never varied. Its size is
33 x N + 1,121 numbers (1,682 at 17 facts, 2,507 at 42); at 42 facts the
first hidden layer (32) is narrower than its input. Running it is cheap
(microseconds); on the Jetson the facts cost the time (3.101: the 25 new
facts 110 ms p90 here, F-large's whole choice 313 ms on the board, bar
100). In 3.101 one new fact carried the gain: `lidar_gain` (unknown cells
visible from the goal by raycast; -14 points of clone agreement without
it).

## Steps

**A. The gate (no build).** Read 3.102 amendment 1's split of F-rich's
mode-R gap to the oracle. As 3.102 wrote before its run: if the **finds**
part is at least the goals and metres parts together, B and C do not
start; the next work is explore's misses (3.96's exhaustions and
livelocks), and this section closes recorded. Otherwise B starts. The user
decides in either case.

**B. Lean facts and a shape sweep.** F-lean: F-large's 17 facts plus
`lidar_gain` (18), the one 3.101 fact that clearly mattered, computed in
numpy. Four shapes, the same 18 facts:

| Shape | Hidden layers | Numbers (N = 18) |
|---|---|---|
| S32x32 (today's) | 32, 32 | 1,715 |
| S64x32 | 64, 32 | 3,347 |
| S32 | 32 | 659 |
| S64 | 64 | 1,299 |

All four are cloned from the oracle (3.96's clone stage, 30 epochs, on
3.101's oracle sets, which already hold all 42 columns). Only the two with
the best held-out clone agreement go on to PPO, at 3.101's budget (60 x 48
x 8, seed 3961), selection as 3.96. The better of the two by mode-R
selection reward goes to the 588 test houses, once. 3.101's F-large and
F-rich test rows are the comparison; nothing re-run.

**Criteria for B (written now, before any build):**

1. **Better than F-large, by the paired test:** a paired bootstrap over
   the test houses (10,000 draws) of mode-R mean reward, the 95% interval
   wholly above 0. (3.101 showed a fixed +0.02 bar is inside the noise; the
   paired interval is what this many houses can resolve.)
2. **Finds:** >= 99% of F-large's, in modes R and U.
3. **Trips:** median goals per found mission no more than F-large's, in R
   and U.
4. **Clean:** 100% of missions end with an outcome under the tick cap.
5. **Cost:** one whole choice on 3.96's benchmark (this machine) no more
   than 1.1 x F-large's p90; the Jetson's 100 ms bar is measured on the
   board when it is reachable.

Reported: all four shapes' clone agreement; the paired house counts (fewer
/ more goals than F-large); U's interval.

**C. Scoring the whole pool (only after B).** Today each goal is scored
alone; the choice is a comparison between goals, which F-rich could only
see through hand-made pool facts. F-pool: each candidate's 18 facts
through B's best per-candidate layers to an embedding, then one
self-attention layer over the pool (every candidate sees every other; 4
heads, width as B's), then one score per candidate. Trained as B (clone,
then PPO at the same budget), test once, numpy forward pass for the board.

**Criteria for C:** B's 1-4 with **B's chosen arm** as the comparison in
place of F-large, and 5 with B's chosen arm's p90 as the base (attention
over ~85 candidates is the cost to watch).

**Out of scope:** anything in `brain/` (records only until the user decides
on wiring); live nav2 runs; the Jetson (owed, as 3.101).

**B as built (2026-10-25, the user: "go ahead with 3.103 step B"; written
before the run).** `evaluations/goal-learn-3103/` (`lean.py`, `run.py`),
3.96's trainer with the shape and the one fact patched in (as 3.101 patched
its features; 3.96's files unchanged):

* **`lidar_gain` is 3.101's computation, cast on the 0.30 m grid** (3.96's
  `coarse`, as its room facts are). The simulator's maps are 0.30 m, so in
  training and test it is 3.101's value exactly (checked: max difference
  2e-8 over oracle choices); on a 0.05 m SLAM map it reads the map as
  training did and takes 10 ray steps, not 60. Cast at 0.05 m, F-lean's
  whole choice was 114 ms p90 against F-large's 93 here (smoke weights),
  over criterion 5's 1.1x; on the coarse grid 98 vs 93 (bar 102).
* **The clone reuses 3.101's oracle sets** (the same 5,464 training houses
  and 37,064 choices; their 42 stored columns sliced to the 18), so all four
  shapes copy the same choices.
* Workers 4 on this container; clone 30 epochs each, then PPO for the two
  best by held-out agreement, at 3.101's budget; then the better by mode-R
  selection to the test houses; criterion 5 on 3.96's benchmark here.

**Read with care:** per house, F-lean takes fewer goals than F-large in 208

against 14.7). Its selection score fell from 0.561 to 0.512 on the test
houses, as E-rich's and F-rich's did: a best-of-seven check on 100 houses
flatters.

**What it means.** The network's shape is not the lever: one layer or two,
659 numbers or 3,347, the copy of the oracle is the same within a point,
and the trained policy lands level with F-large. 3.101 showed more facts of
the same kind are not the lever either. The 0.22 the oracle still holds
over F-rich (3.102) is real, but neither a wider net nor 25 more facts of
today's kinds captures it. **Step C** (scoring the pool together) is next
as written, but the user decides whether to run it, given that B's
per-candidate scorer is already flat across shapes: C changes what the
model can compare (every candidate against every other), which is the one
thing none of the runs so far could test. Records only; nothing in
`brain/` changes.

**C as built: amendment 1 (2026-10-26, the user: "run step C"; written
before the run).** `evaluations/goal-learn-3103/pool.py` (the network, its
numpy forward pass and a check) and `pool_run.py` (the stages), 3.96's
trainer patched as B's was; 3.96's files and B's stages unchanged. What the
plan did not fix:

* **The network.** Each candidate's 18 facts through B's S64 layer (18 ->
  64, tanh) to a 64-wide embedding `e`; one multi-head self-attention layer
  over the pool, **4 heads of 16** (query, key, value and output maps 64 ->
  64 with biases, scaled dot product); `h = e + attention`; score
  `w . f + W3 h + b3`, keeping B's linear term. No layer norm, no
  positional code: the scores follow the candidates when the pool is
  reordered (checked).
* **Masking.** None inside a pool: every candidate attends to every
  candidate of its own pool, itself included. Nothing crosses pools: a
  training batch holds many pools padded to the longest, and padding is
  masked out of the keys. 3.96's clone loss and PPO update score many pools
  concatenated in one call, so `pool_run.py` carries copies of both, line
  for line, that pass the pools' lengths to the network (checked: a batch
  scores each pool exactly as it scores alone, and the numpy pass agrees
  with torch to 2e-7).
* **Initialisation.** torch's defaults under 3.96's seed (3961), except the
  attention's output map, **zero**: the clone starts as an S64 F-lean and
  learns how much to compare. 17,939 numbers (S64: 1,299).
* **Training, as B:** 3.101's oracle sets sliced to the 18 columns, 30
  clone epochs (batch 256, lr 1e-3); PPO at 3.101's budget (60 x 48 x 8,
  seed 3961), selection every 10 iterations on the 100 selection houses,
  the best by mode-R mean reward to the 588 test houses, once.
* **The oracle sets are regenerated** in this container by 3.101's harness
  (the files lived in /tmp). Same houses and code, so the same choices are
  expected; the counts are checked against B's (37,064 training choices,
  609 held out) before the clone, and a mismatch is reported.
* **Criterion 5's base is measured beside F-pool**, in one run of 3.96's
  benchmark on this container (load moves the numbers between runs); B's
  recorded 102.0 ms is reported too.
* **Scoring:** C's 1-4 against B's F-lean rows (`run/test/F-lean-*.json`),
  F-large's and F-rich's paired intervals reported; the gap to the oracle
  (3.102's rows) split into finds, goals and metres, as 3.102 amendment 1.

**Amendment 2 (2026-10-26, the user, during the width-64 PPO and before any
test run): sweep the attention width, then test one.** The user asked
whether C would "try a few sizes, say 32 x 32, 64 x 64 and 128 x 128,
compare them on the houses kept for choosing, and keep the smallest one that
matches the best"; it was not in the plan (C fixed one width, B's), so it is
added here, before the test houses are touched:

* **Widths 32, 64 and 128**, the embedding and the attention alike (4 heads
  each, so heads of 8, 16 and 32): 4,883, 17,939 and 68,627 numbers.
  Everything else as amendment 1 (the same facts, oracle sets, seed, clone
  and PPO budget, selection checks). Width 64 is the run already made in the
  cloud container (arm renamed `F-pool-W64`); 32 and 128 run on one AWS spot
  CPU instance, launched from the user's Mac (`cloud.sh`, 3.96's wrapper:
  checkpoints to S3, a dead-man switch, `down` deletes everything), with the
  oracle sets rebuilt there by the same harness and checked against B's
  counts.
* **The choice, on the selection houses only:** each width's best mode-R
  selection reward (3.96's checks, as every arm); a width **matches** when it
  is within **0.02** of the best width's; the **smallest** width that
  matches goes to the test houses (`pool_run.py choose`). 0.02 because
  selection is 100 houses and its best-of-seven flatters (B: 0.561 on
  selection, 0.512 on test); a smaller gap is not a difference these houses
  resolve.
* **Like for like across hosts:** width 64 was trained in the container,
  32 and 128 on the instance. Before choosing, the instance re-runs W64's
  best weights on the selection houses (`pool_run.py reselect`); the
  missions are deterministic for fixed weights, so its rewards must equal
  the container's within 0.001 (`w64_same_on_host` in `chosen-pool.json`).
  If they differ, `choose` refuses, nothing is tested, and the run goes to
  the user.
* **One test run, of the chosen width only,** on the 588 test houses,
  scored by C's criteria as written (B's F-lean the comparison). The other
  widths are reported by their clone agreement and selection rewards only.
* **Criterion 5** is timed on the instance with nothing else running, F-lean
  and the chosen width in one run of 3.96's benchmark (base measured beside
  it, as amendment 1).
* **Cost:** spot, roughly $1-3 (the user's yes to run it from the Mac,
  2026-10-26); the Mac's Claude Code session takes the run over from here
  (`HANDOFF-2026-10-26-3.103C-pool-width-sweep.md`).
