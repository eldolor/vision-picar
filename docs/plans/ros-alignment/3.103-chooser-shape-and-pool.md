# 3.103 Explore's goal chooser after the ceiling: lean facts, network shape, then scoring the whole pool

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.103; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-25, after 3.101 found F-rich (42 facts) level
with F-large (17): "Should we look at the architecture of the neural net
instead?", then "Add 1, 2, 3 from above to the plan" -- the three steps
proposed in reply, in order:

1. Let 3.102's oracle ceiling (amendment 1) say whether trips have room.
2. If they do: cut to the facts that matter and try a modest sweep of the
   network's shape.
3. Only then: a chooser that scores the whole pool together.

**What is known going in.** Every learned chooser so far (3.65, 3.96,
3.101) is one network: `score = w . f + MLP(f)`, MLP N -> 32 -> 32 -> 1
(tanh), each candidate scored on its own with the same weights. The shape
was set in 3.65 ("about 32 units") and never varied. Its size is
33 x N + 1,121 numbers (1,682 at 17 facts, 2,507 at 42); at 42 facts the
first hidden layer (32) is narrower than its input. Running it is cheap
(microseconds); on the Jetson the facts cost the time (3.101: the 25 new
facts 110 ms p90 here, F-large's whole choice 313 ms on the board, bar
100). In 3.101 one new fact carried the gain: `lidar_gain` (unknown cells
visible from the goal by raycast; -14 points of clone agreement without
it).

## Steps

**A. The gate (no build).** Read 3.102 amendment 1's split of F-rich's
mode-R gap to the oracle. As 3.102 wrote before its run: if the **finds**
part is at least the goals and metres parts together, B and C do not
start; the next work is explore's misses (3.96's exhaustions and
livelocks), and this section closes recorded. Otherwise B starts. The user
decides in either case.

**B. Lean facts and a shape sweep.** F-lean: F-large's 17 facts plus
`lidar_gain` (18), the one 3.101 fact that clearly mattered, computed in
numpy. Four shapes, the same 18 facts:

| Shape | Hidden layers | Numbers (N = 18) |
|---|---|---|
| S32x32 (today's) | 32, 32 | 1,715 |
| S64x32 | 64, 32 | 3,345 |
| S32 | 32 | 659 |
| S64 | 64 | 1,299 |

All four are cloned from the oracle (3.96's clone stage, 30 epochs, on
3.101's oracle sets, which already hold all 42 columns). Only the two with
the best held-out clone agreement go on to PPO, at 3.101's budget (60 x 48
x 8, seed 3961), selection as 3.96. The better of the two by mode-R
selection reward goes to the 588 test houses, once. 3.101's F-large and
F-rich test rows are the comparison; nothing re-run.

**Criteria for B (written now, before any build):**

1. **Better than F-large, by the paired test:** a paired bootstrap over
   the test houses (10,000 draws) of mode-R mean reward, the 95% interval
   wholly above 0. (3.101 showed a fixed +0.02 bar is inside the noise; the
   paired interval is what this many houses can resolve.)
2. **Finds:** >= 99% of F-large's, in modes R and U.
3. **Trips:** median goals per found mission no more than F-large's, in R
   and U.
4. **Clean:** 100% of missions end with an outcome under the tick cap.
5. **Cost:** one whole choice on 3.96's benchmark (this machine) no more
   than 1.1 x F-large's p90; the Jetson's 100 ms bar is measured on the
   board when it is reachable.

Reported: all four shapes' clone agreement; the paired house counts (fewer
/ more goals than F-large); U's interval.

**C. Scoring the whole pool (only after B).** Today each goal is scored
alone; the choice is a comparison between goals, which F-rich could only
see through hand-made pool facts. F-pool: each candidate's 18 facts
through B's best per-candidate layers to an embedding, then one
self-attention layer over the pool (every candidate sees every other; 4
heads, width as B's), then one score per candidate. Trained as B (clone,
then PPO at the same budget), test once, numpy forward pass for the board.

**Criteria for C:** B's 1-4 with **B's chosen arm** as the comparison in
place of F-large, and 5 with B's chosen arm's p90 as the base (attention
over ~85 candidates is the cost to watch).

**Out of scope:** anything in `brain/` (records only until the user decides
on wiring); live nav2 runs; the Jetson (owed, as 3.101).
