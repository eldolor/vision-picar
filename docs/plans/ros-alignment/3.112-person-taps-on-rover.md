# 3.112 A person's goal taps on the Rover teach the learned chooser

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.112; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-27, after the pipeline was laid out as (1)
clone the oracle, (2) PPO on the mission score, (3) a person's corrections
on the real robot: "make it a section now anyway. But we'll cover that
section when the time is right." This section is step 3, **parked**: it
holds the idea, what it builds on and when to open it. Nothing here is
started.

**What a correction is.** A goal tap, never a takeover (the user's
decision, 2026-10-20, recorded in 3.88): explore asks, a person picks one
of the candidates (3.88.A's ask mode, `POST /mission/explore/pick`), and
the pick is a label for the chooser. A person's Stop or D-pad still ends
the mission (3.84) and teaches nothing directly; 3.99's log counts those
and says where they happened, which is where to ask for taps.

**What it builds on.**

* 3.88.A: ask mode, the pick route, the per-choice log, the twin's tap
  (done).
* 3.88.C and D, the same idea in simulation and then on the Rover in the
  user's house, were **dropped** when 3.88.B's criterion 6 failed: ten
  features could not use even the oracle's labels ("reopen with features
  that see more, not with more labels"). Since then the facts went to
  17-42 (3.96, 3.101, 3.110) and PPO took the clones from 0.27-0.40 to
  0.54-0.64 on the selection houses.
* 3.88.B also ran DAgger with the oracle (the learner drives, the oracle
  labels): 0.328 against clone-then-PPO's 0.443, its held-out agreement
  falling from 29% to 24%. A person's taps are DAgger with a person as the
  expert, so that result is the warning to read first.
* 3.99: interventions per hour, the measure of whether taps helped.

**Why not now (2026-10-27).**

* The gap that matters is in simulation, where it is cheapest to close:
  learned choosers 0.55, the oracle 0.78, on the 588 test houses (3.103.C).
  The desktop drives hundreds of missions an hour; the Rover a few, with a
  person watching.
* Taps are few: about five choices a mission, so a day gives about a
  hundred labels, against 37,064 oracle choices in the clones' sets.
* What would need correcting on the Rover is not known until a learned
  chooser runs there: the gap from simulation to the real house
  (perception, odometry, rooms that look different) is the failure taps
  can fix that simulation cannot, and nobody has seen it yet.

**When to open it (both):**

1. A learned chooser runs on the board as explore's goal choice (3.110's
   timing work is the step towards that), and
2. real missions show it choosing in ways simulation does not explain:
   3.99's interventions per hour, or the per-choice log, worse on the
   Rover than the same chooser in simulation.

**When opened, first:** criteria written before any tap is recorded
(CLAUDE.md section 7), at least: the measure (the real house's reward, or
3.99's interventions per hour, before and after N tap missions); how the
taps are used (added to the clone's set, step 1, or as a correction PPO
starts from, step 2); what share of choices a person is asked about, and
how that is chosen (3.99's intervention sites, or the chooser's least
certain choices).

**Out of scope until opened:** everything; no code, no data.
