# 3.83 Explore wedged under the dining table (2026-10-15): built; FAILED, not adopted

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.83; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.70 M5's live half FAILED: from an empty map, `explore` found
the backpack in 10 of 13 rooms of `home_first_floor`. Hall, laundry and
living room ran out their 1800 s (`evaluations/unsurveyed-370/`).

**Diagnosis (2026-10-15, from the runs' brain and robot logs in
`/tmp/vision-picar-3.70/run-20261014-{212046,220450,223704}/`).**

* **It is a wedge, not a goal loop.** M5's record guessed that explore
  cycled frontier goals because failures were counted per point. Wrong:
  `RetryBook` already counts them within 0.6 m. The goals are different
  because the robot never reaches any of them. In the living-room run,
  from 183 s on every goal -- whatever its point -- timed out at 120 s
  with the robot moved 0.01-0.38 m, at the same pose, (8.4-8.5,
  9.9-10.1) m. Explore's escape (REVERSE 0.3 m, turn 90 deg) worked
  each time, and the next goal drove the robot straight back in.
* **Where.** Under the dining table: on ground truth the chassis stands
  14-19 cm from two table legs, cells (26, 32) and (29, 32), with a chair
  at (28, 31) behind them -- inside the 20 cm stop. The robot server
  refused 14,161-24,936 forward commands per run ("forward clamped:
  19.8cm <= 20cm (scan_footprint)"). nav2's plan goes through; the
  safety layer will not let the chassis follow it. The same class as
  3.67's garage exit.
* **Only the failed runs.** Goals that failed within 0.7 m of the spot:
  hall 15 (1,021 s), laundry 6 (722 s), living room 14 (1,685 s); the ten
  found runs 0. Hall and living room went under the table right after the
  foyer; laundry got there at 401 s.
* **3.68 saw the spot.** Its refusal keepout placed marks at (8.2, 9.5) m
  during explore (its record, criterion 7). It was not adopted: goal 6
  still failed, and a mark did not free a robot the controller kept
  driving forward (40-61 marks a run, re-fired at one spot). Its record
  lists what would have to change: mark once per spot, and an action the
  controller cannot override when a mark does not help.

**Direction (the user, 2026-10-15).** The safety layer may veto the
planner because it has the latest fused sensor data; it is right, and it
stays exactly as it is. The fix gets what it knows to the planner: 3.68's
refusal keepout, with the two changes 3.68's record named (option B; a
brain-only guess from the brain's older map was option A, not taken).

**Design.**

1. **3.68's keepout, ported onto today's `dev`.** `robot/wedge_watch.py`
   (3 s of forward clamps on driver `ros` is a wedge), the robot server
   marking a disc 0.30 m ahead (r 0.15 m, 300 s, at most 8), the bridge's
   `POST /nav/keepout` drawing them into a latched mask, and nav2's
   `KeepoutFilter` in the **global** costmap only (3.68 amendment 1), now
   in `nav2.yaml` itself. The bridge's route budget goes 13 -> 14.
2. **Mark once per spot.** A wedge whose spot lies within
   `SPOT_DEDUPE_M` (0.30 m) of a live spot sends nothing (3.68: one spot
   re-marked up to 30 times evicted useful marks and meant nothing).
3. **Back out when a mark does not free it, in the brain.** A mark
   reroutes the planner, but a robot already wedged is held by the
   controller pushing forward (3.68's record). Explore ends a goal early
   as `stalled` when the robot has moved less than `STALL_M` (0.15 m)
   over the last `STALL_S` (20 s) of it, and its existing wedge handling
   runs: cancel, back out (REVERSE, creep, turn to open space), choose
   again -- now on a plan that routes around the spot. No new movement
   path: the brain's escape verbs go through `/action` and the safety
   layer as today (rule 4), and the robot server only draws a mark.
