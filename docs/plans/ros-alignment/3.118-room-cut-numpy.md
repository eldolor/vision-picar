# 3.118 The map's room cut in numpy, exactly the old answers

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.118; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.70.S2's board timing (2026-10-28, `c580a30`): `brain/room_labels.py`
`segment()` takes 1.45 s (`home_first_floor`), 2.22 s (`procthor:val:9`) and
2.77 s (`procthor:val:50`) at 15 W on fully mapped houses at the car's 0.05 m
cells, and a survey's labeller runs it every 5 s (`RESEGMENT_S`) on the
mission thread, so the brain waits 29-56% of the time once the house is
mapped. 3.96's `rooms` goal policy calls it too, on its coarser grid (41 ms on
the board, 3.109). The user, 2026-10-29: option 1, "make it faster" (numpy,
the same rooms), over moving it off the thread.

Profile on the desktop (cProfile, the three maps): `clearance()` -- a
Dijkstra with a Python heap -- is 62-65% of the cut; the rest is the
watershed's Python loop over every free cell, the tiny-piece merge and the
renumbering.

**The change (written before building).**

* `clearance()` becomes a vectorised relaxation: the same sources (occupied
  cells at 0, the map's edge cells at 0.5) and the same eight steps (1.0 and
  1.4142), each pass taking `min(d, shifted d + step)` over whole arrays
  until nothing changes. Dijkstra's answer is, for each cell, the least
  left-to-right float sum over all paths; floating-point addition of a
  positive step is monotone, so relaxation to a fixed point reaches that
  same least sum, bit for bit (by induction along the best path, each
  prefix sum is reached and none lower is possible). Criterion 1 checks it.
* The watershed loop keeps its order and rules -- cells widest first
  (stable), the four neighbours in the same order, the same set of roots
  sorted by peak, the same merge test -- but runs over flat Python lists on
  a map padded by one cell, so it does no numpy scalar indexing and no
  bounds checks. The tiny-piece merge counts touches with array operations
  and keeps `Counter.most_common`'s tie rule (first touch in the old loop's
  order). The renumbering is already numpy.
* No caller changes. `RESEGMENT_S` stays 5 s.
* Out of scope: moving the cut off the mission thread; caching the cut per
  map version.

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **The same answers, exactly.** Today's `segment()` and `clearance()` are
   frozen as `tests/room_cut_reference.py`. The new `segment()` returns a
   `seg` array equal to the reference's element for element and a clearance
   array equal bit for bit (`np.array_equal`, no tolerance), on: the suite's
   own test maps; the simulator's houses fully mapped at their own 0.30 m
   cells and at 0.05 m (3.70's car-resolution maps, 1 m unknown margin);
   ProcTHOR `val` 0-99 at 0.30 m and `val` 0-19 at 0.05 m; the final maps of
   9 in-process sim surveys (`tests/survey_sweep.py`'s houses); and 500
   seeded random grids (free, occupied and unknown mixes, sizes 1 x 1 to
   200 x 200, including all-free, all-occupied, all-unknown and one-row
   maps), at `NECK_RATIO` and at two other ratios. 0 differences.
   (Amendment 1, written before those were compared: the six live 0.05 m SLAM maps
   of 3.77's benchmark, `evaluations/goal-choice-377/maps/`, are added;
   and instead of only the surveys' final maps, every cut the labeller
   makes during the 9 `spacing` surveys is compared as it is made.)
2. **Faster on the board:** by 3.70's method (the board as it stands, 15 W,
   the board lock, the brain's venv, one warm call then 20 timed), the
   median on each of the three car-resolution maps is at most a third of
   3.70's record: `home_first_floor` <= 0.483 s, `procthor:val:9` <=
   0.739 s, `procthor:val:50` <= 0.923 s.
3. **Nothing else moves:** the full suite (`ready`) passes, every pinned
   survey and rooms-policy sweep unchanged.
4. **Live:** one survey against the local stack (plain sim, robot server
   and brain, `home_first_floor`, through the brain's HTTP API, a 300 s
   `survey_time_limit_s`) ends without a fault, with rooms labelled and 0
   contacts.
