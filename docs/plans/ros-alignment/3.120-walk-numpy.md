# 3.120 spacing's walking distance in numpy, exactly the old distances

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.120; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.118 made the room cut 7.5-8.1x faster on the board, and left
`brain/room_labels.py` `_walk()` -- `spacing`'s walking distance, a
4-neighbour Dijkstra with a Python heap -- as the larger part of a survey's
pause: 0.352 s (`home_first_floor`), 0.614 s (`procthor:val:9`) and 0.791 s
(`procthor:val:50`) on the board at 15 W with 20 seeds at 0.05 m
(2026-10-29), against the cut's 0.18-0.37 s, together ~11-23% of a survey's
time once the house is mapped. On 2026-10-29 the other per-tick candidates
were measured on the board beside its running services (the map's encode
~11.5 ms and decode ~8 ms a fetch, the camera's seen cells 5-7 ms, the
pivot guard 4.4 ms turning by a wall): none is a pause of this size. The
user (2026-10-29, going to bed): "I will go with whatever you recommend next
that needs to be optimized".

`_walk()` has two callers. `_wants()` -- every cut, and every new seed, on
the mission thread during a survey -- reads only the distance. `layer()`
reads the distance and the owner (which seed reached a cell first); it runs
for a find mission's labels on 3.96's coarse grid, and on a survey's map only
when `room_labels()` is read, never per tick.

**The change (written before building).**

* A new `_walk_dist(seg, sources, res, door_m)` returns `_walk()`'s distance
  array alone, by 3.118's method: a relaxation that revisits only the cells
  that changed, over a flat array padded by one cell of non-floor. The same
  sources (each snapped by `_floor_near()`, unchanged), the same four steps,
  and the same float expression per step, `(d + res) + door` with door
  `door_m` where the two cells' rooms differ and `0.0` where they match.
  Each cell ends at the least left-to-right float sum over every path, which
  is what the Dijkstra gives, since adding a positive step is monotone in
  floats (3.118's argument). Criterion 1 checks it.
* `_wants()` calls `_walk_dist()`. `_walk()` stays exactly as it is, for
  `layer()`: its owner follows the heap's pop order on ties, and an exact
  vectorised copy of that is not worth the risk for a path that is not per
  tick.
* Out of scope: the owner; moving the cut or the walk off the mission
  thread; `RESEGMENT_S`.
