# 3.74 Below the lidar: a floor band and floor hazards

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.74; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Status: planned 2026-10-12 (the user: "borrow whatever you can and add it
to the plan").** Nothing built. Parts A, B and D can run before the Rover;
C's accuracy and every `[car]` criterion need real frames or the car.

**Why.** The D500 sees one plane, about 12 cm off the floor (3.27). A shoe,
a sock, a cable or a pet lying flat sits under it, and on the car nothing
else looks there: `HardwareRobot.get_depth_grid()` answers "unusable", so
`robot/safety.py` runs on the scan alone. Section 6 question 8 proposed a
floor band from the OAK-D Lite's depth and was never built. Ecovacs solved
the same problem on a robot vacuum
([TensorFlow blog, 2020-01](https://blog.tensorflow.org/2020/01/ecovacs-robotics-ai-robotic-vacuum.html)):
a front camera and a small detector for the things its laser and bumpers
miss, on top of its laser SLAM. This section takes 6.8 forward and borrows
what transfers from that article.

**Where each half sits (rule 3, section 1.1).** The floor band feeds a
veto, so it lives in the robot process (`depthai`, not `depthai-ros`) and
in `robot/safety.py`, in series with the cone and the swept corridor. The
hazard class is recognition: it can be wrong, so it **steers** and never
vetoes. It runs in the brain with the rest of perception, and reaches
motion only as goals the brain avoids and (under `drive: ros`) marks nav2
plans around. A false hazard costs a detour; a false veto costs a stop.

## Part A -- the simulator gets heights

Today every object fills the lidar's slice (`GridWorld.solid_cells`), and
`MockRobot.get_depth_grid()` publishes `rows: 1` from the same 2D
raycaster. Nothing low can exist, so nothing in B or C can fail red.

* Objects get an optional `height_m` (default: tall, so every house and
  test is unchanged). `get_scan()` ignores an object lower than the lidar
  plane (`LIDAR_Z_M`, from the xacro); it stays solid to the body, so
  driving onto it is a ground-truth contact.
* The depth grid gains a **floor-band row**: per zone, the nearest low
  object within the OAK-D's geometry (level camera ~10.2 cm up, 58 deg
  vertical field, MinZ 20 cm with 400P + extended disparity; the numbers
  6.8 read from the vendor, constants with their source). Inside MinZ the
  zone reads `unusable`, never clear (M3).
* `complex_house` gets a variant with low hazards (`SIM_HAZARDS=1`):
  cables, socks, shoes on the floor along the tour and explore routes.
* The sim's synthesised detections (`GridWorld._detections()`, 1.12)
  report low hazards through a **stand-in detector** with seeded recall
  and false-positive rates, set from C1's measurement once it exists
  (until then: 0.8 recall, 0.05 false-positive frames).

**Criteria A:**

1. With no `height_m` anywhere, scans and depth grids on all four houses
   are identical to `dev` over a recorded 500-step drive (a pinned test).
2. A 5 cm object in front of the robot is absent from `get_scan()` and
   present in the floor-band row at its true range +/- one cell, from
   MinZ out to 1.5 m.

**Design, as built (no criterion changed).**
* The band is a `floor` block inside the depth grid, not a second row:
  it is another sensor at another pose (body-fixed, 73 deg), and a row
  would inherit the level row's `pan_deg` and `fov_deg`.
* Heights go by label (`GridWorld.object_heights`), so `move_object()`
  and the split sim's shared state need nothing new.
* The hazard house is a house name, `SIM_MAP=complex_house_hazards`, not
  `SIM_HAZARDS=1`, so the split sim's sensor programs build the same
  heights from the house name they already share. Besides the six
  hazards, ten of 3.46's small things are under the plane there (shoes,
  slippers, keys, remote, toy car, mug, pet bowl, towel, headphones,
  book); `complex_house` itself is unchanged.
* The stand-in detector is on when `SIM_HAZARD_RECALL` or
  `SIM_HAZARD_FP` is set.

**Found on the way:** the band first reported its own 1.5 m horizon as a
range when nothing was there; a test caught it and the reach is now
tested on the ray, not on the clearance.

**Review (`/code-review`), three findings, all fixed:** the band drew its
noise from the level row's random stream, so a seeded noisy run in a
house with no heights would no longer repeat (now its own stream; the test
fails red without the fix); noise could carry a band reading past its
declared 1.5 m; the low cells were rebuilt on every fake-lidar beam (now
cached against the object table, which is replaced, never mutated).

## Part B -- the floor band in `robot/safety.py` (6.8 (a)-(b))

`path_clearance()` already takes the depth grid's path zones by bearing.
The floor band is that grid's lower row, read through the same cone, so
`forward_clearance()` keeps one shape: cone, corridor and floor band in
series, the nearest wins. M8's floor rejection is what keeps the floor
itself out of the band: on the car, a depth return counts only if it
stands more than `FLOOR_MARGIN_CM` above the plane the camera's height and
tilt predict. Forward only: the band does not cover the sides while
pivoting, reverse, or the last ~14 cm inside MinZ (6.8 says so; the lidar
and low speed remain the only protection there).

**Criteria B (6.8's (a)-(c), with numbers):**

1. **Low objects stop the robot, on ground truth.** 3.18's sweep method
   (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`) with a 5 cm object across the corridor,
   many starts, 0.1 and 0.2 m/s: every run stops with travel-to-contact
   **>= 18 cm**. Red first: the same sweep with the band off makes
   contact in some runs.
2. **No cost to progress.** On the furnished home with no low objects,
   the tour and parked numbers are no worse than 3.67's adopted config
   (tour >= 8 of 9, longest park < 120 s); with S5 sensor noise on,
   floor-band vetoes with no low object within 50 cm of the path: **<= 1
   per 10 minutes** of driving.
3. **`[car]` A real stop and a quiet floor.** Shoe (~10 cm) and folded
   sock (~3 cm) across the path, tape-measured: stop **>= 18 cm** from
   the bumper, 5 of 5 each, on each floor type in the house. Ten minutes
   of driving per floor type with nothing low on it: **0** floor-band
   vetoes. The flat cable is measured and recorded, not barred: depth is
   expected to miss it, and that is C's job.
4. **`[car]`** The OAK-D runs 400P with extended disparity (Waveshare's
   stock config does not, 6.8), and the band's sensor age is counted in
   `_aged()` like the lidar's.

**Amendment 1 to criterion B2, written 2026-10-12 before any B2 run.**
"Floor-band vetoes per 10 minutes of driving" has no clock in the
in-process instrument, and a band return off a wall the lidar also sees is
not a false veto. B2 is measured in two halves:

* **B2a, in process.** 3.18's forward sweep (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s
  starts: the four standard houses, which have nothing low, 24 headings,
  0.1 and 0.2 m/s), with the band on and off, each with S5's noise off
  and on (sigma 3 cm, the yaml's; dropout 0.2, the twin demo's -- the
  yaml's is 0). Every difference the band
  makes there is spurious by construction. Bars, with the band on:
  (i) 3.18's criterion 1 holds (no run under 18 cm after a move);
  (ii) 3.18's progress share (runs starting >= 60 cm from contact that
  cover >= 25 cm) is no more than **1 point** below the band-off run with
  the same noise; (iii) runs where the band stops the robot **more than
  3 cm** earlier than without it: **<= 1%**.
* **B2b, live, unchanged:** the furnished-home tour, three runs, on the
  3.67 adopted config: tour >= 8 of 9, longest park < 120 s, coverage
  median >= 0.83 at 900 s.

**Why (iii) fails under noise, and what it says.** S5's model draws an
independent sigma-3 cm error for each zone, and the band's answer is the
nearest zone in the corridor -- so the more zones, the further the minimum
falls below the truth. The same criterion read **1.8% with 32 zones and
10.2% with 96**: the number measures the noise model's zone count, not
the band. With exact sensors the band changes no run. S5's sigma is an
HC-SR04's, applied per zone; a stereo camera's error is per pixel, grows
with range, and is reduced by our own driver (min, or a low percentile,
over many pixels). The sim cannot predict the car's false-stop rate;
**B3 (on the car, each floor type, ten minutes, 0 floor-band vetoes) is
the measurement that decides it**, and if the car fails it, the first
lever is the driver's reduction (a low percentile instead of the min),
not the safety rule.

**Design changes made after the B2 pilot and before the measured runs;
no bar changed.**
1. Each zone publishes the bearing of its range (`bearing_deg`): placed
   at the zone's centre, a wall just outside the corridor read inside it
   (pilot, exact sensors: progress -2.5 points, 1.6% early).
2. The band's ranges are from the lens (`origin_x_cm` 6.5), not the
   bumper: from the bumper, a 30 cm corridor's side walls read as in the
   path.
3. 96 single-ray zones: one centre ray per 9-degree zone let a sock's
   corner fall between rays (B1 first draft: two of 100 runs at 17.7 cm).

**Review (`/code-review` and the Thermos bug pass), all fixed before the
measured runs above:**
* **Fusion with the lidar removed** (both reviewers). A band return
  with a scan return within 2 cm was left to the scan, but that scan
  return could lie just outside the corridor, where nothing judges it: a
  low object at a chair leg's foot was excused. Under noise it fused
  almost nothing anyway (B2a (iii) 1.8% with it, measured first).
* **A creep can drive into the band's blind range** (Thermos) --
  **recorded as a known gap, not fixed.** The band reads nothing inside
  its 20 cm minimum range (about 14 cm past the bumper); a creep (3 cm
  bar, 3.44) after the band stopped the robot can drive the sock into that
  range and on into it. Not a regression: before 3.74 nothing saw the
  sock. A hold (the last hit carried by the signed wheel travel) was
  built and passed its red-first test, then the second review removed it:
  a farther hit overwrote it, `/depth` raced it with no lock, it failed
  open on a feedback blip, and the robot server's fallback controller and
  the brain's own each kept a separate one. Options for the car: refuse a
  forward creep while the band is fitted (costs the creep escape forward),
  or keep the hold in ONE place on the robot server with a lock.
* Beside the body, the band now counts only inside the chassis' width,
  as the scan does (R6's frozen-at-a-jamb lesson); a zone's nearer
  return can no longer hide a farther one (single-ray zones); a null
  `bearing_deg` falls back to the zone centre instead of raising inside
  the veto; one scan and one grid per decision (and per `/depth`, which
  also passes its grid to the veto); the band and the scan share one
  corridor rule (`_corridor_room()`); the floor block's fields are
  documented on `RobotInterface`.
* Known gaps, not fixed: the band does not see the sides of a pivot or
  astern, so a turn can swing a low object into the blind range unseen
  (the lidar and low speed are the protection there, as 6.8 said).

## Part C -- the hazard class (steering, never a veto)

* **Classes:** `cable`, `sock`, `shoe`, `pet bowl`, `toy` -- YOLOE prompts,
  the open-vocabulary path already in `brain/perceive.py`. CLIP is not
  needed; a hazard need not be identified, only avoided.
* **Per-class thresholds,** tuned for recall: chosen on half of C1's
  labelled frames, scored on the other half.
* **Placement.** A hazard sits below the lidar, so 3.46's rule ("the
  lidar measures, never a monocular guess") cannot apply. Order: the
  OAK-D's depth inside the box; else the floor-contact range (camera
  height h and tilt, the box's bottom edge at angle theta below the
  horizon: range = h / tan(theta)), marked as such; never the sim's own
  answer.
* **Use.** Hazards go into a short-lived hazard list in world frame
  (`brain/`, beside the inventory; they expire, since socks move). Explore
  and frontier goals within the chassis' half-width plus 10 cm of a
  hazard are rejected. Under `drive: ros` the brain sends them as keepout
  spots in nav2's global costmap, reusing 3.68's keepout plumbing
  (`plan/3.68-refusal-replan`, unmerged; its review fixes come with it).

**Criteria C:**

1. **`[real frames]` Detector accuracy.** A hazard recording session:
   the floor-height rig (Stage 0's method), the five classes placed along
   real routes, at least two lighting groups (D1). On the held-out half,
   per class within 1.5 m: recall **>= 80%** (cable, sock, toy) and
   **>= 90%** (shoe, pet bowl); hazard-free frames with any hazard box:
   **<= 5%**. A class that fails is dropped from the list, not re-tuned on
   the test half.
2. **`[real frames]` Range.** Floor-contact range against a tape, 0.3-1.5
   m: median error **<= 10%** of the range. `[car]` The same against the
   OAK-D's depth on the boxes where both exist.
3. **Steering works, in sim.** `complex_house` with `SIM_HAZARDS=1`,
   the stand-in detector at C1's measured rates (0.8 / 0.05 until then),
   the same seeds with the hazard list on and off: ground-truth contacts
   with low hazards fall by **>= 80%**; missions' find rate falls by
   **<= 5 points** and median time to find rises by **<= 15%** (the
   progress half, section 7.4).
4. **The wall holds.** No hazard path reaches the robot server except as
   goals and keepout spots through the bridge (`tests/test_wall_linters.py`
   unchanged); R6 6/6 on the scaled house with the keepouts on.
5. **One mission through the live stack** (brain HTTP API, `drive: ros`,
   hazards on) ends with an outcome and no hazard contact.

## Part D -- perception data, borrowed practice

No new models, no cloud calls; all on the labelled corpus that exists.

1. **Lighting.** `PUT /recording/walks/{walk}/meta` takes `lighting`
   (`daylight`, `backlit`, `lamp`, `dim`); `perception_eval score`
   reports each group beside the total. Existing walks are tagged from
   their frames, marked as tagged after the fact. A group more than 10
   points under the total is reported as a finding.
2. **Robustness to floor colour and light.** On the pinned frame set:
   hue +/- 15 deg, brightness +/- 30%, contrast +/- 20%. The bar: the
   gate's decision (P >= 0.8, target / not) flips on **<= 5%** of frames
   per shift. Recorded either way; a failure is a finding for the
   Rover's frames, not a reason to tune here.
3. **Near-duplicates.** `perception_eval` gains `--dedup` (consecutive
   frames whose downscaled difference is under a stated threshold);
   both counts and both rates printed. No bar: it tells us how much of a
   rate came from a robot standing still.
4. **By distance.** Recall reported in bands (< 0.5, 0.5-1.5, > 1.5 m),
   from the lidar range at the frame where the walk has one.
