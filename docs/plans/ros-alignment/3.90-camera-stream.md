# 3.90 The camera as a stream, and frames combined over time

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.90; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user asked (2026-10-21) whether navigating from video instead
of photos every 500 ms would be more accurate, and whether the Rover can
stream. The answer agreed the same day:

* **The 500 ms is the phone's pace, not the robot's.** It is the twin's
  `GUIDANCE_THROTTLE_MS` / `ROBOT_THROTTLE_MS` (`web-twin/app.js`). The
  brain ticks every `tick_interval_s` = 0.25 s, and on the Jetson at 15 W
  local perception takes 61 ms median, 110 ms p90 a frame (3.33), so it
  could run at about 9 frames a second inside its 250 ms budget.
* **Both Rover cameras stream** (the pan-tilt USB camera and the OAK-D
  Lite's colour sensor). **The car has no camera driver yet:**
  `robot/hardware_robot.py` `get_camera_frame()` raises "no camera driver
  yet" and defers to `sensors`. So the driver can be built as a stream at
  no extra cost: a reader keeps the newest frame, and `get_camera_frame()`
  returns it instead of opening the device for each photo.
* **Video is not more accurate by itself.** It is the same frames at a
  higher rate. Accuracy improves only where frames are *combined*: a k-of-n
  vote against one-frame detector flicker, and the sharpest of several
  frames during a turn. It does not help the cloud's identity check
  (Opus 4.5, 67.9% precision on 3.48's frames): Claude reads still images,
  so "video" there is N paid calls for one answer. The cloud stays on
  triggers, one still frame a call. Obstacles stay the lidar's and the
  depth band's.

**What already combines frames.** `tier_consecutive_frames` = 2 (a
`candidate_sighting` needs two frames) and `ARRIVAL_FRAMES` in
`brain/arrival.py` (the target centred within the radius on consecutive
frames). Both are **counted in frames**, so a faster perception rate
silently shortens them (R1c: thresholds counted in steps change meaning
when the step does). Step A must keep their meaning in seconds.

**Out of scope.** Sending video, or more frames, to the cloud. A local
video-native VLM (single-frame local VLMs already sit at the 0.5 GB memory
floor, 3.78/3.80). Object tracking across frames beyond a vote, unless C's
data says the vote is not enough. The OAK-D's stereo depth (3.74's floor
band, 3.41 part B).

**Cost.** No Bedrock call is planned in any step. If D's live check on the
Rover runs tiered missions, their cloud calls are asked for first, with an
estimate (the user's rule).

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

*A. The stream driver (testable now, on a fake source and the Jetson).*

1. **Freshness.** With a 30 fps source, the age of the frame `GET /frame`
   returns (capture stamp to reply) is **p90 <= 100 ms** over 10 minutes,
   measured on the Jetson at 15 W beside the full stack.
2. **No stale picture.** A source that stops delivering makes `/frame`
   answer 503 within **1.0 s**, never an older frame (`TeleopRobot`'s
   rule). An unplugged and replugged source delivers again within **3 s**,
   with no restart of the robot server.
3. **It costs the robot nothing.** The reader runs off the event loop:
   **0 late wheel-loop ticks** in that 10-minute run (3.87's measure), and
   the reader uses **<= 10% of one core** at 1280-wide JPEG (3.37's
   headroom check).
4. **Counted rules keep their time.** At perception rates of 4 and 9
   frames a second, `candidate_sighting` and arrival need the same span in
   seconds as today's two frames at the 0.25 s tick: at least 0.25 s, and
   no more than one frame interval beyond it. *(Amended 2026-10-21 before
   building: "+-0.05 s" cannot be met at 9 fps, whose frames fall 0.111 s
   apart, so a run spans 0.222 s or 0.333 s.)* Only frames that carry a
   capture stamp (`captured_at`, the stream's and the phone's) are timed; an
   unstamped frame (the simulator's) is counted exactly as today. Pinned in
   a test, red first against today's counts. *(Amended 2026-10-21 after
   review, before any measurement on the car: the lower bound is 0.25 s
   less `SPAN_SLACK_S` 0.05 s, i.e. 0.20 s. A 30 fps camera read at the
   0.25 s tick gives stamps 0.217-0.283 s apart, so a 0.02 s slack made
   most of the car's two-frame runs wait a third tick. Only the sighting
   and arrival runs are timed; the band, out-of-vocabulary, cold-search
   and stale counts stay in frames, which equal ticks while perception
   runs once a tick.)*
5. **Nothing else changes.** On the simulator (no stream: frames as
   today), the 100-house sweep still pins 99 and the tiered sim criteria
   in 3.80 are unchanged.

*B. A pilot on frames that exist (no Rover, no cost).*

6. On the labelled rig walks (frame set pinned by hash before running;
   P24's rule), k-of-n over consecutive frames at the existing gate
   (P >= 0.8), for (k, n) in {(2,2), (2,3), (3,5)}, against single frames.
   **Bar:** at least one (k, n) raises per-frame precision by **>= 5
   points** with recall down **<= 2 points**. These frames are 500 ms
   apart, so less correlated than a 9 fps stream: B chooses which (k, n)
   C measures, it does not decide adoption. A failed B is recorded and C
   still runs, on all three.
   *(Amended 2026-10-23 before running B: the frame set and what it can
   show.)* The frames are 3.92's: the 2,261 frames of the 21 labelled walks,
   with the shipped pipeline's per-frame result already recorded
   (`evaluations/tier-calibration-392/perception.jsonl`, sha256
   `944fabe5...0737dbf`, CPU only; P24). B re-runs no model: the vote is
   computed over those rows, in each walk's frame order. A frame is a
   positive under (k, n) when it is itself detected and at least k of the
   last n frames (itself included, fewer at a walk's start) are, the rule
   `brain/tiered.py` `_tr_candidate` applies; the rows carry no capture
   stamps, so the window is counted in frames, not in 0.25 s ticks. Read
   before running: a single frame there is already 426 of 431 precise
   (98.8%), and the vote only removes positives, so precision can rise by
   at most 1.2 points: **criterion 6's +5 cannot be met on this set.** B
   is run anyway for the recall cost of each (k, n) and for which false
   positives a vote removes; it is recorded as failed.

*C. The Rover's own frames (waits on the Rover; shares 3.80.U's
recording).*

7. Walks recorded from the stream at 9 fps, labelled as 3.48's were.
   **Vote bar:** the same as criterion 6, on these frames.
8. **Sharpest frame bar:** during pivots, the sharpest of the frames since
   the last perception pass (variance of the Laplacian) raises detection
   recall by **>= 5 points** against the newest frame, with precision down
   **<= 2 points**.

**The Jetson's rate is the ceiling (the user, 2026-10-21: no other
hardware will be bought).** B and C replay recorded streams offline, but
only at frame rates the Jetson sustains beside the whole stack, simulated
by subsampling the recording: today ~5 fps (3.80.P, perception p90 171 ms
with VE resident at pace 1), re-measured on the Rover with step A's
driver. A setting that needs more frames than that is not eligible,
whatever it scores. The replay's value is a fixed, repeatable frame set
for comparing settings, and finding the LOWEST rate that meets the bars
(fewer frames leave CPU, GPU and memory to the rest of the stack); it is
not a preview of faster hardware.

*D. Wired in, only what C met.*

9. **Progress:** live tiered missions (sim 3/3, then the Rover in the
   house) end `found` at least as often as without it, with **0 contacts**.
10. **Stability, paired with 9:** paid `candidate_sighting` calls per
    mission fall by **>= 30%**, and no mission makes more paid calls than
    its baseline.

*E. The same on the twin's Drive via brain (the user, 2026-10-21: "enable
this feature on the Digital Twin as well so that I can validate it
manually").* The phone is the camera here: `getUserMedia` already streams,
and today each 500 ms tick snapshots it, pushes the frame
(`POST /teleop/frame`), then polls `/mission/status`, so frames arrive at
the round trip's pace. E decouples the two. It is a manual check plus a
source of real stream-rate frames for B and C before the Rover; it decides
no adoption (D does, on the Rover's data).

11. **Frames as a stream.** Pushes leave on their own timer at a set rate
    (Settings: 2 / 4 / 8 fps, default 4), one in flight, newest wins
    (a slow upload skips frames, never queues them). Status polling stays
    at 500 ms. Measured on the laptop stack through the tunnel: achieved
    push rate >= 90% of the set rate at 4 fps over 5 minutes.
12. **Decisions on the latest frame; recording on every frame.** The
    brain's perception always takes the newest frame (`GET /frame`, as
    today), never a backlog: "the next unseen" would let a slow brain fall
    up to the whole buffer behind (16 frames at 4 fps is 4 s) and act on
    the past. *(Amended 2026-10-21 before building, the user's question:
    the first draft pulled the next unseen frame.)* Recording is a
    separate reader: `TeleopRobot` keeps the last 16 pushed frames, and the
    brain's recorder drains them by seq (`GET /teleop/frames?after=<seq>`)
    off the decision path. Frames the perception did not take are counted
    in status (`frames.skipped`) and shown on the HUD
    (`FPS 3.8 / skip 2%`).
13. **The vote, behind a switch.** Settings: Combine frames Off (default) /
    2 of 3 / 3 of 5, sent at start (`frame_vote`), applied to
    `candidate_sighting` only, over the frames perception actually took
    within a window in seconds (criterion 4), so a skipped frame neither
    votes nor breaks the run. Status and the HUD show the votes
    (`VOTE 2/3`). Off is today's behaviour exactly (pinned).
14. **Recording at stream rate, uploaded once.** With Record this walk on
    while driving via brain, the **brain** saves every frame the robot
    server received (not only the ones perception took), each with its
    seq and `perceived: true|false`; a perceived frame also carries its
    perception result and the decision made on it. A recorder more than
    16 frames behind loses frames, counted in the walk's manifest
    (`frames_lost`), never silently. The phone stops uploading each frame
    a second time (`POST /recording/frame`). Over a
    5-minute walk at 4 fps: >= 95% of pushed frames recorded, 0 duplicate
    seqs, and `walk_eval` / `walk_replay` / `perception_eval` read it
    unchanged. Recording without Drive via brain is unchanged.
    *(Amended 2026-10-21 before building, on reading the readers: "read it
    unchanged" holds for `walk_replay` and `perception_eval`, which join
    frames to decisions on `teleop_seq` / `last_frame_seq` already;
    `walk_eval` counts every row's action, so it scores only rows that are
    not `perceived: false`, or a stream walk would read as hundreds of
    frames without a move. The per-walk cap, `MAX_FRAMES_PER_WALK` 500, is
    two minutes at 4 fps: a walk the brain records from a stream may hold
    5,000 (20 minutes at 4 fps); a walk the phone posts keeps 500. The
    walks land in the brain's own store, as the phone's do today.)*
15. **No extra spend.** Paid cloud calls per minute do not rise with the
    frame rate (vision: one per tick; tiered: triggers counted in seconds,
    criterion 4). Measured on a sim-backed run: 2 vs 8 fps within 10%.
16. A UI test at 390 px, red first, and a phone-size screenshot. The
    twin is deployed only with the user's yes.
