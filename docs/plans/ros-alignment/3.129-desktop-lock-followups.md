# 3.129 The desktop lock's open items: pushes skip the queue, long jobs step aside, the nightly takes it

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.129; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.128's two open items (the user's go, 2026-10-31, on the fixes
proposed for them): the nightly's full suite (timed) held only its own
lock; and a push (shared) queued behind a queued live run, and so behind
whatever that run waited for -- a long training job, for hours.

**Design.**
* **A. A push skips the queue.** `acquire(..., queue=False)` (shared only)
  does not pass the gate, so a queued exclusive job does not hold it up; it
  still waits for an exclusive HOLDER. The fast tier (`ready`, the push
  gate) uses it. The queued job starts a few minutes later at most, and
  pushes are too rare to starve it.
* **B. Long shared jobs step aside.** `Hold.step_aside()` /
  `desktop_lock.step_aside()`: if an exclusive job is queued (the gate held
  across three probes 0.1 s apart -- with a shared hold in place only an
  exclusive acquirer waits there), let go, wait for it through the gate,
  take the lock back. The goal-learn PPO loops (`evaluations/goal-learn-396/train.py`
  `stage_ppo`, `evaluations/goal-learn-3119/credit.py` `stage_ppo`) take
  the lock shared with `hold_for_life` and call it before each iteration.
  Under a wrapper (a parent's hold) it is a no-op.
* **C. The nightly takes it.** Its driver acquires exclusive before
  pytest, retrying every `NIGHTLY_LOCK_RETRY_S` (30) for
  `NIGHTLY_LOCK_WAIT_S` (7200), stops still heard; then the night reads
  SKIPPED (exit 126, no email). The wait is outside the time limit;
  pytest gets the token so the live tests' own locks pass through; the
  hold is released after pytest. A commit without the tool runs unlocked.
  The nightly is installed on the Mac (launchd), not this desktop.

**Criteria** (the three behaviours the user approved; written down during
the build, not before it -- recorded as such):

1. An unqueued shared job passes a queued exclusive one and never runs
   beside an exclusive holder; the fast tier asks for it, `--full` does not.
2. A shared `hold_for_life` job calling `step_aside()` lets a queued
   exclusive job run, takes the lock back only after it, does nothing when
   none is queued, and is a no-op under a parent's hold.
3. The nightly: a free desktop runs the suite holding exclusive (pytest
   sees an `exclusive:` token) and lets go after; a desktop busy past the
   wait is SKIPPED with no email and no pytest; a holder finishing within
   the wait lets the night run; a stop during the wait reads STOPPED within
   10 s; a commit without the tool runs unlocked; 3.76 J's nightly tests
   unchanged.
4. Each of A and B's tests fails with its change removed.
5. `ready` passes.

Found while building: the driver first kept its hold to exit, leaving its
note; it now releases after pytest (and on a stop before it), and
`status` prunes dead holders' notes even when the lock is free.

**Known limits.** A job with no steps (a docker build) cannot step aside;
a job started under a wrapper cannot either (the parent holds the lock).
The PPO loops step aside between iterations (minutes), not mid-iteration.
An old-style exclusive holder (no gate) is invisible to `step_aside()`.
