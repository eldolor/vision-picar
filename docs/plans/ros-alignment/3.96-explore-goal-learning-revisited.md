# 3.96 Explore's goal choice, learned again: richer features (rooms, indirect search) toward the oracle's ceiling

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.96; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-23: "we need to revisit this again, add it to the
plan" -- the learned goal choice (3.65), which never became the default.
What is known:

* **3.65 phase 2:** the PPO network met every bar on its test set (-27%
  median goals) but on 613 fresh houses saved only 11% (bar 20%), while
  finding 2 more (608 vs 606). `explore_goal_policy` stays `rule`.
* **3.88 B:** the ceiling is far away. An oracle that knows the true floor
  path to the target needs a median of **3 goals against the rule's 9**
  (600 fresh houses). Cloning it through the same ten features agrees only
  29% (bar 60%), and as badly on its own training data (25.9%): the ten
  features **cannot tell the oracle's candidate from the others**. Clone
  then PPO cut goals 22% but found 590 vs 596. Its conclusion: "reopen with
  richer features, not more labels."
* **The ten features are all geometry** (path, turn, size, unknown share
  near the candidate, retries ...). None says which room a candidate opens
  onto, whether that room was already entered, or where this kind of
  object usually is. That last one is ASR's indirect search, which the user
  agreed to add as a candidate the same day; here it enters as a feature.

So this section adds features that carry the information the oracle uses,
measures first whether they can tell its pick apart, and only then trains.
Everything else in explore stays as 3.65 left it: only the choice among
candidates the rules already allow is learned.

**What the new features are** (computed in the brain from what it has; a
candidate's room comes from the map the brain sees, never from ground truth):

* **Room features:** whether the candidate lies in, or opens onto, a room
  explore has not entered; the share of that room still unknown; whether
  the candidate is through a doorway (3.70's `segment()` and doorways).
* **Indirect search (a prior):** how likely the target's kind of object is
  in the candidate's room type, learned once from the **training** houses'
  own object placements (ProcTHOR rooms carry a type and their objects).
  The room type comes from the brain's room labels (3.70's
  `brain/room_labels.py`); in the simulator they are the house's room names
  as the stand-in labeller, with an arm that drops labels (below).
* **Context:** how many candidates share the candidate's room (a cluster of
  frontiers in one unexplored room vs a lone sliver).

**Houses -- never mixed, as 3.65 and 3.88:**

* **Training:** ProcTHOR `val` usable 300-569 and `train` usable 0-299
  (the latter in 3.88's test pool, so it never tests again here).
* **Selection:** `val` usable 100-199.
* **Test, run once at the end:** **ProcTHOR `train` houses 1000-1999**, a
  new import no section has seen (3.66's import checks first). The rule,
  3.65's network and the new policy run on the same houses.

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **The features carry the signal (step A, before any training).** A
   classifier trained on oracle picks in the training houses, using the
   old ten features plus the new ones, picks the oracle's candidate on
   **at least 50%** of held-out choices (selection houses), against the
   ten features' 29.1% on the same choices. If this fails, stop: no
   training run, the result recorded.
2. **Fewer goals, no fewer finds (the test houses):** found **>= the rule's
   minus 1%**, and median goals per found mission **>= 20% lower** than the
   rule, or median metres **>= 20% lower**. The same bar as 3.65 and 3.88,
   unchanged.
3. **Robust to missing room labels:** with the brain's room labels dropped
   at random on **50%** of rooms (on the car 3.70 S3 named 46 of 90
   rooms), criterion 2's found bar still holds and the goal saving is
   **>= 10%**.
4. **No house lost unexplained:** every test house the rule finds and the
   new policy does not is classified in this entry.
5. **Clean:** 100% of missions end with an outcome under the tick cap.
6. **Fast enough on the Jetson:** a whole goal choice (candidates,
   features, scoring) p90 **<= 100 ms** at 15 W on 3.65's six live maps
   (3.77's bar; the rule's is 90 ms).
7. **Live, on nav2:** behind `explore_goal_policy: <new name>` (default
   `rule`), three `explore` missions in `home_first_floor` on the live stack
   end `found`, with no more contacts than the rule and median time no more
   than 20% over it.
8. **The hand-drawn houses hold** with the flag on; with it off nothing
   changes. The test-house numbers are pinned in a test.

**Out of scope:** learning on the car; anything below the goal choice;
the found and arrival rules; turning explore into a behavior tree (its own
section -- the learned policy would be one leaf in it either way); cloud
calls (none are needed: room labels in the sim come from the house).

**Known confound, recorded before measuring:** 3.79 found that a slower
goal choice alone cut live goals 29 -> 22 with time unchanged. The sim
sweeps use `FakeNav` and are not affected; criterion 7's live goal counts
are read with that in mind, and time is its bar, not goals.

**Amendment 1 (2026-10-23, before any measurement).** Two facts found while
building A:

* **The sweeps' target is placed in a uniformly random room**
  (`sim/maps/procthor.py`: a seeded choice among the rooms other than the
  start's). A room-type prior can learn nothing from that. ProcTHOR's own
  floor objects, though, follow its generator's room rules, the same on
  all three splits (`val`, measured): basketball and baseball bat 100% in
  bedrooms, plunger 100% bathroom, garbage bag 100% kitchen, cart 100%
  living room, laundry hamper 86% bathroom, dog bed and box about 55/45
  bedroom/living room, house plant about a third in each of three, garbage
  can 55% bathroom. So there are **two target modes**, run separately,
  never pooled:
  * **U (uniform):** today's `red backpack`, unchanged. Only the room
    features can help here; the prior is flat by construction.
  * **R (real objects):** the target is one of the house's own floor
    objects, a category drawn (seeded by the house index) from those it
    has, out of this fixed mix: basketball, plunger, garbage bag, cart,
    laundry hamper, dog bed, box, house plant, garbage can. Every instance
    of that category counts as the target. A house whose drawn category
    the start cannot reach draws again; one with none of the mix is
    recorded and left out. The mix holds sure, split and spread
    categories, so a prior that only memorises "always a bedroom" cannot
    win. This is ProcTHOR's world, kinder than a real house; criterion 3's
    dropped labels and the U arm are the checks against leaning on it.
* **The ProcTHOR sweeps carry no room labels** (explore reads a map's
  `room_labels` only after a survey). In the simulator a **stand-in
  labeller** gives the type of the true room under a candidate once at
  least **25%** of that room's floor is known on the brain's map (seen by
  the lidar, so a room seen through a doorway can be named, as a camera
  would); before that it is unknown. Labels feed the features only; they
  are **not** written into the map, so explore's own room goals (3.70 E3)
  stay off and the rule is the rule.

**The new features** (all from the brain's map, its own pose trail and the
labeller; `brain/goal_policy.py` `room_features`): from `segment()` of the
brain's map -- the candidate's segment never stood in, its area, a
different segment from the robot's, the share of the pool in the same
segment; from the labeller -- the type known, the prior P(category | type)
learned from the **training** houses only, and prior x never-entered.

**Criterion 1, made exact:** judged on mode **R**'s held-out choices
(oracle-driven missions in the selection houses), strict agreement (the
oracle's own index, 3.88's measure), by 3.88's linear clone (softmax over
the candidates, L2 1e-3) on the ten old features plus the new ones; a
one-hidden-layer network (32 units) on the same inputs is also run, and
either reaching **50%** meets it. The ten-feature clone is re-fitted and
scored on the same R choices as the baseline (3.88's 29.1% was mode U).
Mode U is reported beside it.

## A results (2026-10-23): criterion 1 FAILED; B and C not started

Oracle-driven missions (`evaluations/goal-learn-396/a.py`, 3 min 37 s on
the laptop, CPU): mode R, training 570 houses (545 `found`, 8 with no
reachable target of the mix, left out), 2,140 choices; selection 100
houses (99 `found`), 313 choices. Mode U: 567/570 and 100/100 `found`,
1,768 and 296 choices. The prior (`prior.json`, training houses only)
reads as amendment 1 expected (garbage bag: kitchen 1.0; basketball:
bedroom 0.53 a room, others 0; red backpack flat, 0.16-0.23).

**Criterion 1, strict agreement with the oracle on held-out choices
(`a.json`):**

| Mode | Features | Linear | Network (32) | Bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R** | old ten | 22.4% | 26.5% | -- |
| **R** | old ten + room | **28.4%** | **31.0%** | **50% -- FAILED** |
| U | old ten | 28.7% | 30.7% | (reported) |
| U | old ten + room | 30.7% | 34.1% | (reported) |

Tie-aware (the learner's pick costs the same as the oracle's) adds about a
point (R + room linear 29.7%). The rule's own pick is the oracle's on
14.4% (R) and 15.2% (U). On training choices the network reaches only
41.0% (R + room): underfitting again, not overfitting.

**Where the room features help:** on R's sure categories (basketball,
plunger, garbage bag, cart; 136 held-out choices) the linear clone goes
from 25.7% to **36.0%**; on the split and spread ones (177) from 19.8% to
22.6%. So the prior carries real signal where the house's rules are
sharp, and the room features add 2-6 points everywhere, but nothing comes
near half.

**What it means.** The oracle picks by the true path to where the target
*is*. With the target in an unentered room, several candidates usually lead
into that room or the next (`room_share` averages 0.38), and which one the
oracle takes depends on the target's exact position, which no fair feature
can know. Agreement with the oracle may be the wrong yardstick for a policy
that cannot see the target: a policy can save goals while disagreeing with
it often. That is a reason to change the measure by an amendment written
before any run, not to read this one as met. As written, **A failed and the
section stops here**; B and C are not started. `explore_goal_policy` stays
`rule`; nothing in `brain/` changed.

## Amendment 2 (2026-10-23, the user, before any of its runs): judge on goals saved, more houses, the map itself

A failed as written and stays recorded as failed. The user chose three
changes ("I like one, two, and three"):

1. **More training houses.** 3.65 and 3.88 trained on 270 houses, and
   3.65's network lost more than half its gain on unseen ones (-27% ->
   -11%). ProcTHOR's `train` split has 10,000; only its first 1,000 are
   imported (`TRAIN_HOUSES`). All 10,000 are imported (the same pinned
   file and hash; the first 1,000's usable list is unchanged), 3.66's
   import checks run on the new ones. **Large training set:** usable `val`
   300-569, usable `train` 0-299 and every usable `train` house from index
   2000 on. The test houses stay `train` 1000-1999; selection stays `val`
   100-199.
2. **The map itself as an input.** Beside the 17 features, each candidate
   gets a crop of the brain's map around it: 32 x 32 cells at 0.2 m (6.4 m
   square, resampled from the map's own resolution so the SLAM map's 0.05 m
   and the sim's cells read alike), rotated so the robot-to-candidate line
   points up, with channels free, occupied, unknown, seen by the camera, the
   robot's trail, and the labelled room's prior. A small network (two
   convolutions, then the 17 features joined, one score out) ranks the
   candidates. Trained in torch on the laptop; on the Jetson it runs in
   numpy (the brain imports no torch for this) and must meet criterion 6.
3. **Judged on goals saved, not on matching the oracle.** Criterion 1 is
   no longer a gate. Training is 3.88's: clone the oracle as a starting
   point, then PPO on 3.65's reward (+1 found, -0.02 a goal, -0.01 a metre),
   unchanged.

**Arms, each trained the same way and to the same budget** (PPO
iterations and missions fixed in the harness before the first run):

* **F-small:** the 17 features, 3.88's 570 training houses (A's set).
* **F-large:** the 17 features, the large training set.
* **MAP-large:** the 17 features plus the crop, the large training set.

F-small against F-large isolates the data's effect, and F-large against
MAP-large the map's.

**One arm goes to test.** Each arm is scored on the selection houses only
(mode R, then U), and the arm with the highest mean reward on R goes to
the test houses, once, beside the rule and 3.65's network. The others are
never run on the test houses.

**Criterion 2, made exact for the two target modes:** on the test houses
the chosen arm must, **in mode R**, find >= the rule's minus 1% and cut
median goals per found mission by >= 20% (or median metres by >= 20%);
**in mode U**, find >= the rule's minus 1% and send no more median goals
than the rule. Criteria 3-8 are unchanged.

**Amendment 3 (2026-10-23, the user, before any run): a PONI-style fourth
arm.** PONI (Ramakrishnan et al., CVPR 2022) learns where to look from
top-down maps by supervised learning, scoring **every** frontier rather
than copying one pick, which is the failure A measured (several
candidates leading into the target's room). **PONI-large:** MAP-large's
network and houses; its clone stage, instead of the softmax over the
oracle's pick, fits each candidate's score by binary cross-entropy to a
potential from the oracle's true path cost: `1 - min(1, (cost - best) /
10 m)` (best = the pool's lowest cost; unreachable = 0). PONI's area
potential is left out (the reward's goal and metre costs stand in for it).
Then the same PPO budget as the other arms. The clone's own selection
score (iteration -1) counts as a candidate for its best, so PONI's
interaction-free version is measured too. The choice of arm for the test
houses is unchanged (highest mode-R mean reward on the selection houses,
now over four arms).
