# 3.69 Explore's misses in the imported houses

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.69; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.66 imported 1,000 ProcTHOR floor plans and ran `explore` over
the first 100 usable ones: found 84, bar 90, FAILED. None of the 16 misses
was an import fault. They fall into four classes of `explore` behaviour
(3.66's entry has the houses and the evidence):

* **A, 8 houses:** the target seen only while the camera was panned;
  `_sighting()` ignores a panned frame and nothing turned the body back.
* **B, 4 houses:** a good sighting, but the approach point the furniture
  left reachable is beyond the 0.40 m arrival radius, and nothing closes
  the last few centimetres.
* **C, 2 houses:** the lidar ranged the wrong surface at the sighting's
  bearing (the target at the frame's edge), so the approach went to the
  wrong place.
* **D, 2 houses:** never in any frame, though coverage counted the floor
  round it as seen. Not explained yet.

The user asked (2026-10-11) for a section that fixes them, judged on the
same 100 houses with the bar still at 90.

**The risk is tuning to these 16 houses**, the same fault 3.66 found in
four hand-drawn ones. So the fixes are judged on a second hundred houses
nobody has looked at, measured before any change, and the hand-drawn houses
keep every number they pin today.

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

`FakeNav` sweeps as in 3.66 (`tests/procthor_sweep.py`, `max_steps` 200).
The **tuning set** is usable houses 0-99 (3.66's); the **held-out set** is
usable houses 100-199, measured once before any code change and not
examined house by house until the fixes are final.

1. **Found on the tuning set >= 90 of 100** (3.66's bar, unchanged).
2. **The held-out set does not get worse, and reaches the bar:** found
   after >= found before, and >= 90 of 100. No house found before may be
   lost unless its new miss is classified in this entry.
3. **Every sweep ends cleanly:** 100% of both sets with an outcome, no
   exception, under the tick cap.
4. **Progress, not just outcome** (section 7.4): on houses found both
   before and after, in both sets, the median goals sent per mission rises
   by no more than 10%.
5. **The hand-drawn houses keep their numbers:** the full suite passes,
   every existing explore test and pinned sweep number unchanged
   (`tests/test_explore*.py`, the house sweeps on `SLOW_FILES`).
6. **Each class is answered:** for A, B and C, a test that fails on `dev`
   and passes after (red first). For D, the cause is found and either
   fixed with a red-first test or recorded with the evidence.
7. **Through the real path, once:** one `explore` mission on the live
   stack (robot server + brain over HTTP + the ROS container, nav2) in
   `home_first_floor` ends with an outcome; the outcome is recorded.

If a bar fails, it is recorded as failed and pinned at the measured value,
as 3.66 did.

**Note on criterion 2.** The held-out set was measured before any change
(85) and first examined after the fixes were final (100/100). The code review
then changed four rules, and two held-out houses (270, 319) were diagnosed
to get the review's version right. That made the held-out set no longer
unseen, so a **third hundred (usable 200-299), never examined, was measured
before and after: 84 -> 99**, nothing lost, its two tick-cap runs gone. Its
one miss (house 378) is not diagnosed.

**What was wrong, and the fix (`brain/explore.py`, `brain/frontier.py`):**

* **A, a panned glimpse (8 houses).** `_glimpse()`: the target in a PANNED
  frame is a direction, not a place; explore centres the camera and turns
  the body toward it, and the next centred frame decides.
* **B, a refused turn to face it (4 houses).** The 3.66 entry read these as
  "stopped beyond the 0.40 m arrival radius"; the traces show otherwise. The
  sim's found rule is the target within 0.9 m and in view, and in every B
  house the robot stood 0.6-0.67 m away with the target out of view, its
  turn to face refused by the pivot guard (3.19) -- and then dropped the
  look, or (house 101) re-sent the same approach until `max_steps`. Now a
  refused turn toward the target is retried at half the angle down to
  `FACE_MIN_TURN_DEG` (10 deg); then, once per place, REVERSE, turn and a
  guarded FORWARD; if that fails too, the sighting counts a retry failure,
  so going back to it runs out of tries.
* **C, the lidar past the target (2 houses).** At the sighting's bearing the
  lidar window held two surfaces (house 125: 1.97, 2.2, 3.76, 3.76, 3.76 m)
  and the median took the wall behind the backpack. On an edge
  (`ArrivalCheck._is_edge`) the nearest return is taken when it lies
  beyond the approach band (0.7 m): it is short of the target, never past it.
  Inside the band the median is used if it is inside the band too (most
  beams on the target); otherwise no range: the nearest may be a door jamb
  (read as "already there") and the median the wall behind; another frame
  decides.
* **D, never in a frame (2 houses).** Two causes. **108:** the place a view
  goal stood the robot to look at an unseen patch was chosen by straight
  line to the patch's middle, behind a wall from it; the patch was then
  marked looked at. `find_view_gaps()` now takes the best-ranked place with
  a line of sight to the patch (facing its middle, else its nearest part).
  **27, not fixed:** the kitchen's only way in from the explored side is a
  0.6 m passage between a counter and a wall. The Rover (0.25 x 0.23 m) fits,
  but no cell in it leaves the 0.3265 m goal clearance 3.45 requires, so no
  goal is ever sent into it and the kitchen stays unmapped. Relaxing 3.45's
  clearance is a decision of its own (its tour numbers depend on it).

**The review** (`/code-review`, 6 findings) changed four rules before the
final measurement: only a turn toward the target backs off (not one toward
an unseen patch); the queued FORWARD is dropped if the turn after backing
off fails; the edge range is used only beyond the approach band (a jamb at
arm's length read as "at the target"); a glimpse never displaces a queued
escape. A second review (2 findings) split a glimpse from the target: its
point is a direction 1 m out, so a refused glimpse turn may back off but never
fails a place in the retry book or drops a real sighting; and it found the
first edge fix put the median back for a target inside the band (now no
range). A third (2 findings): inside the band the median is kept when it
agrees with the nearest return (a close target with one beam past it), and
a glimpse backs off at most once from where the robot stands. Every sweep
was re-run after each review; the table is the final
code. The first review's cost finding was measured, not changed: `find_view_gaps()` on
a full-size 5 cm map takes 0.04-0.05 s. Red-first tests:
`tests/test_explore.py` (the 3.69 block, 13 tests). The sweep test now pins
**99** (`tests/test_procthor_sweep.py`).
