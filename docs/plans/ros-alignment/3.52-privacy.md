# 3.52 Privacy: what leaves the house, and blurring people before the cloud

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.52; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** Idea 5 of the 2026-10-09 comparison with XPENG/Banma's
"on-device first, cloud-assisted" cockpit, which names keeping data local as
a benefit. This robot photographs the inside of a home, yet the specs gave
only cost and latency as reasons for calling the cloud on triggers alone.
The user chose (2026-10-09): **write privacy into the policy spec now; MEASURE
whether blurring people before a frame goes to the cloud fits the perception
budget and leaves the cloud's answers unchanged, before adopting it.** The
default stays off in this section whatever the numbers say; turning it on is
the user's call.

## Part A: the spec (done in this section)

`docs/policy/ARCHITECTURE.md` gains a decision, "Privacy is a reason for the
trigger discipline", and an inventory of what leaves the house, traced to the
code (see the spec for the table).

## Part B: what the corpus holds (measured before the criteria)

The labels (`labels.json`) record only the target, never people. Three
passes, 2026-10-09, laptop (MPS):

1. **Detector prepass over all 3,198 frames of the 29 local walks:** YOLO11s
   (COCO `person`) and YOLOE-11s prompted `person`, both at confidence 0.25,
   imgsz 640. 150 frames fired. Every hit was looked at on contact sheets.
   **149 are false:** duvet corners, sofa cushions, a cable-knit pouf, chair
   and table legs, a vacuum, a backpack, sneakers standing empty on the
   floor. One is a person (below).
2. **That one hit led to a scene the detectors otherwise missed:** a person
   lying on a sofa under a red blanket in the basement, 3-6 m away, dimly
   lit. Every frame of the six 2026-09-08 basket walks (625 frames) was then
   looked at, then every candidate at 640 px.
3. **Result: 43 frames clearly show that person, plus 4 where only the
   blanket shows** (excluded from both sides). One person, one scene:

   | walk | frames |
   |---|---|
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-212419 | 0066-0078 (13) |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-212549 | 0052-0062 (11) |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-215140 | 0042-0052 (11); 0053-0054 blanket only |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-215252 | 0041-0048 (8); 0049-0050 blanket only |

   The other 23 walks were checked only where a detector fired, so their
   person count (0) is a lower bound. **Seen before the criteria were
   written, and so a description, not a test:** at confidence 0.25 / 640 px,
   YOLO11s marked the person on 1 of 43 frames and YOLOE on 0.

**Data gap, whatever the criteria read:** 43 frames of one reclining person
is not a corpus for a privacy feature. What would close it: a floor-height
rig walk (CLAUDE.md section 5's rig) through two or three rooms with a person
**standing, walking, seated and lying**, at 1-5 m, in daylight and lamp
light, with the walker's own feet in frame for part of it -- the views a
floor robot actually gets of people.

## Part C: criteria (written 2026-10-09 before any of these runs)

The detector arms not yet run: **YOLOE-11s prompted `person` and YOLO11s
COCO `person`, each at confidence 0.05 (the tier's `LOW_CONFIDENCE`) and at
imgsz 640 and 1280** -- four arms. A frame counts as a hit when a box
overlaps the person (checked by eye on every hit).

1. **Recall (the gate):** the best arm marks the person on **>= 90%** of the
   43 person frames.
2. **False blur:** that same arm fires on **<= 5%** of the 578 person-free
   frames of the same six walks (625 - 43 - 4).
3. **Budget, Jetson at 15 W, the 60 pinned `bench_frames.json` frames:**
   a person pass run on every frame adds **<= 25 ms p90** and keeps the
   tier's p90 **<= 250 ms**; or, run only on frames about to go to the
   cloud, adds **<= 10%** of the cloud call's median latency.
4. **Cloud answers unchanged:** on up to 60 frames the chosen arm would blur
   (person and false hits alike, since a false blur over an obstacle is the
   harm), the deployed `/navigate` (its default model) answers the blurred
   and the original frame with the **same `action` on >= 90%** and the
   **same `target_visible` on >= 95%**. Cost cap about $5; estimate first.

Adoption needs all four. Failing criterion 1 ends the case for blurring on
the shipped detectors whatever 2-4 read; they are still measured where cheap,
because criterion 4's false blurs are the cost of any future arm.
