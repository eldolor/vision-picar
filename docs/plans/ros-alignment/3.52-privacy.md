# 3.52 Privacy: what leaves the house, and blurring people before the cloud

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.52; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** Idea 5 of the 2026-10-09 comparison with XPENG/Banma's
"on-device first, cloud-assisted" cockpit, which names keeping data local as
a benefit. This robot photographs the inside of a home, yet the specs gave
only cost and latency as reasons for calling the cloud on triggers alone.
The user chose (2026-10-09): **write privacy into the policy spec now; MEASURE
whether blurring people before a frame goes to the cloud fits the perception
budget and leaves the cloud's answers unchanged, before adopting it.** The
default stays off in this section whatever the numbers say; turning it on is
the user's call.

## Part A: the spec (done in this section)

`docs/policy/ARCHITECTURE.md` gains a decision, "Privacy is a reason for the
trigger discipline", and an inventory of what leaves the house, traced to the
code (see the spec for the table).

## Part B: what the corpus holds (measured before the criteria)

The labels (`labels.json`) record only the target, never people. Three
passes, 2026-10-09, laptop (MPS):

1. **Detector prepass over all 3,198 frames of the 29 local walks:** YOLO11s
   (COCO `person`) and YOLOE-11s prompted `person`, both at confidence 0.25,
   imgsz 640. 150 frames fired. Every hit was looked at on contact sheets.
   **149 are false:** duvet corners, sofa cushions, a cable-knit pouf, chair
   and table legs, a vacuum, a backpack, sneakers standing empty on the
   floor. One is a person (below).
2. **That one hit led to a scene the detectors otherwise missed:** a person
   lying on a sofa under a red blanket in the basement, 3-6 m away, dimly
   lit. Every frame of the six 2026-09-08 basket walks (625 frames) was then
   looked at, then every candidate at 640 px.
3. **Result: 43 frames clearly show that person, plus 4 where only the
   blanket shows** (excluded from both sides). One person, one scene:

   | walk | frames |
   |---|---|
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-212419 | 0066-0078 (13) |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-212549 | 0052-0062 (11) |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-215140 | 0042-0052 (11); 0053-0054 blanket only |
   | woven-laundry-basket-20260908-215252 | 0041-0048 (8); 0049-0050 blanket only |

   The other 23 walks were checked only where a detector fired, so their
   person count (0) is a lower bound. **Seen before the criteria were
   written, and so a description, not a test:** at confidence 0.25 / 640 px,
   YOLO11s marked the person on 1 of 43 frames and YOLOE on 0.

**Data gap, whatever the criteria read:** 43 frames of one reclining person
is not a corpus for a privacy feature. What would close it: a floor-height
rig walk (CLAUDE.md section 5's rig) through two or three rooms with a person
**standing, walking, seated and lying**, at 1-5 m, in daylight and lamp
light, with the walker's own feet in frame for part of it -- the views a
floor robot actually gets of people.

## Part C: criteria (written 2026-10-09 before any of these runs)

The detector arms not yet run: **YOLOE-11s prompted `person` and YOLO11s
COCO `person`, each at confidence 0.05 (the tier's `LOW_CONFIDENCE`) and at
imgsz 640 and 1280** -- four arms. A frame counts as a hit when a box
overlaps the person (checked by eye on every hit).

1. **Recall (the gate):** the best arm marks the person on **>= 90%** of the
   43 person frames.
2. **False blur:** that same arm fires on **<= 5%** of the 578 person-free
   frames of the same six walks (625 - 43 - 4).
3. **Budget, Jetson at 15 W, the 60 pinned `bench_frames.json` frames:**
   a person pass run on every frame adds **<= 25 ms p90** and keeps the
   tier's p90 **<= 250 ms**; or, run only on frames about to go to the
   cloud, adds **<= 10%** of the cloud call's median latency.
4. **Cloud answers unchanged:** on up to 60 frames the chosen arm would blur
   (person and false hits alike, since a false blur over an obstacle is the
   harm), the deployed `/navigate` (its default model) answers the blurred
   and the original frame with the **same `action` on >= 90%** and the
   **same `target_visible` on >= 95%**. Cost cap about $5; estimate first.

Adoption needs all four. Failing criterion 1 ends the case for blurring on
the shipped detectors whatever 2-4 read; they are still measured where cheap,
because criterion 4's false blurs are the cost of any future arm.

**Amendment 1 (2026-10-09, after criteria 1-2 were measured and before any
criterion 4 call):** criterion 4 also asks the cloud the ORIGINAL frame a
second time, as a control. `/navigate` is a sampled model and can disagree
with itself on the same pixels; without the control, "blurring changed the
answer" mixes in the model's own noise. The bar is unchanged and is judged on
blurred-versus-original; the control's agreement is reported beside it. To
keep the cost cap with three calls a frame: **40 frames** (every one of the
chosen arm's person-frame fires, the rest an evenly spaced sample of its
false fires), 120 calls, estimated at about $2.30 (Opus 4.5 on Bedrock,
$5 / $25 per million tokens in / out, about 2.7k in and 0.2k out a call).
The chosen arm is the one with the best verified recall.

## Part D: measured 2026-10-09

**Criteria 1-2** (`python -m tools.person_eval`, laptop MPS, the 625 frames
of the six walks; every person-frame box then checked by eye, a hit only if
it overlaps the person):

| arm | fired on person frames | verified hits (recall) | false fires (of 578) |
|---|---|---|---|
| YOLOE-11s `person`, conf 0.05, 640 | 2 | 1 (2.3%) | 31 (5.4%) |
| YOLOE-11s `person`, conf 0.05, 1280 | 15 | **7 (16.3%)** | 122 (21.1%) |
| YOLO11s COCO `person`, conf 0.05, 640 | 6 | 3 (7.0%) | 48 (8.3%) |
| YOLO11s COCO `person`, conf 0.05, 1280 | 3 | 2 (4.7%) | 60 (10.4%) |

YOLOE @ 1280's misses-that-fired were a wall poster (5), the gaming chair,
a floor pillow and the ceiling; most of its "hits" frame the person's head
through the rocking chair's spindles, not the person. **Criterion 1 FAILED
(best 16.3%, bar 90%). Criterion 2 FAILED for the chosen arm (21.1%, bar 5%);
no arm meets it.**

The first run of the tool read YOLOE as 0 fires everywhere: it tested the
checkpoint's full path for a `yoloe` prefix and so loaded YOLOE as plain YOLO
with COCO class 0. Fixed (`is_yoloe()` reads the file name) and pinned in
`tests/test_person_eval.py`; the table is the second run.

**Criterion 3** (Jetson, 15 W, the 63 pinned `bench_frames.json` frames,
GPU, scratch bench run from `/tmp`, nothing installed):

| | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|
| YOLOE `person` pass, 640 | 60.0 ms | 64.1 ms |
| YOLOE `person` pass, 1280 | 108.1 ms | 143.3 ms |
| blur + JPEG re-encode (1280's boxes) | 19.6 ms | 29.1 ms |

Every frame: +143 ms p90 (1280) or +64 ms (640) against a 25 ms bar --
**fails**. Only on frames about to go to the cloud: 143 + 29 = 172 ms p90
against 10% of the cloud call's median (3,048 ms, n = 9, the tier stats of
`red-backpack-20260921-203152`) = 305 ms -- **met**, so criterion 3 is MET
by the on-trigger arrangement. (The bench's own tier p90, 361 ms, is not a
regression: it built a pipeline per target and warmed only the first three
frames, so cold starts reached the p90. 3.33's warmed p90 is 110 ms.)

**Criterion 4** (the deployed `/navigate`, its default model; YOLOE @ 1280's
boxes; 40 frames = its 15 person-frame fires + 25 evenly spaced false fires;
3 calls a frame):

| | blurred vs original | control: original vs original |
|---|---|---|
| same `action` | **34 / 40 (85%)** | 38 / 40 (95%) |
| same `target_visible` | **38 / 40 (95.0%)** | 40 / 40 |

**`action` FAILED (bar 90%); `target_visible` met at the bar exactly (95%).**
By frame type: on the 15 person frames blurring changed the action on 2,
and the control changed it on the same 2 (the model's own noise). On the 25
false fires blurring changed the action on 4 and the control on 0, and both
`target_visible` flips are false fires where the "person" box sat **on the
woven laundry basket itself** (`212719/0006`, `215351/0005`): the blur hid
the search target from the cloud. The harm is the false blurs.

Run 1 of this criterion read `action` from the wrong field (it is the
scene's `safest_direction`, not `_navigate.action`) and recorded none; its
`target_visible` agreed with run 2 (38/40, control 40/40). Both runs cost
240 calls, about $4.60 by the estimate above.

**Verdict.** Criteria 1, 2 and 4 failed; 3 met (on-trigger only). **Blurring
people is not adopted, and no blur code is built** -- a flag over a detector
that misses people and erases targets would be a privacy claim the robot
cannot keep. The spec records the decision (Part A). The default question
is moot; nothing changes on the car.

**What would reopen it:** the rig walk in Part B (people standing, walking,
seated and lying at 1-5 m, two light levels), and a detector judged on it
before any blur is wired. A floor-level view of people is mostly legs and
feet; a detector trained on that view, or a pose model, are the candidates
to try first.
