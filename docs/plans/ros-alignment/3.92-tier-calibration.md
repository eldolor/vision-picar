# 3.92 Calibrating the tiers on the labelled corpus: when to trust Qwen, when to ask

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.92; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** After 3.91 B6 the user asked whether the tiered rules could be
learned. `docs/evaluations/LEARNING-APPROACHES-2026-10-22.md` puts two
approaches first, and neither needs a simulator or a paid call:

* **Calibration plus value of information (approach 1).** Each tier's raw score is mapped
  to a probability that the target is in view, so "trust it or ask" becomes
  a comparison of expected gain against the price of a question.
* **A learned router (approach 3).** It predicts, frame by frame, when the local VLM's
  answer is wrong, so the cloud is asked only then.

The user also asked (2026-10-22) whether replaying every walk through GPT-6
Astra is worth it. The answer was "not now": Astra's one strength is steering
with the target in view (56/60 against YOLOE + CLIP's 44/60 in 3.80.I), and
whether that slice is large enough to pay for is measured here, free, first.

**The data.** The 21 walks with an adjudicated `labels.json`
(`recordings/<walk>/labels.json`, `target_visible_labels`): 2,261 frames,
535 with the target in view, six objects (a white charger cable, a blue
bottle, blue and yellow running shoes, a grey backpack, a red / burgundy
backpack, a woven laundry basket). These are phone frames on the floor rig
(CLAUDE.md section 5), not the Rover's camera, so every number here is
re-checked on the Rover's walks (3.80.U's harness).

**What is run (free; no Bedrock call).**

* **The local VLM:** VE (3.78) through `service/local_vlm` on the Jetson at
  15 W. The frame question is the live one: `/identity` with `box: true`,
  the target words from `labels.json`'s `description`. The
  `picar-local-vlm` unit is started for the run and stopped after, with the
  stack left as it is (as 3.91 B6).
* **Perception:** the shipped pipeline (`pipeline_for`, YOLOE-11s-seg crops
  -> CLIP RN50) on the laptop's CPU, every frame. Its best crop's P, its box
  and its status are recorded. All perception rows come from one device
  (P24).

**Criteria (written before measuring; CLAUDE.md section 7).** "Held out"
always means **leave one object out**: a fit on five objects, scored on the
sixth, for each of the six. A row is never scored by a fit that saw its
object.

1. **The data is complete.** A VE answer and a perception row exist for
   all 2,261 frames. A VE answer is either `answered` with a score, or a
   status that is not `answered`. Recorded, no bar: the VE p50 and p90 per
   question, and VE's yes / no / unable counts at today's thresholds
   (`LOCAL_YES_AT` 0.5, `tier_local_no_below` 0.1).
2. **Calibration.** Each tier's score is mapped to P(target in view) by
   Platt scaling (a logistic fit on the score's logit; two numbers per
   tier), fitted held out. It is scored by the expected calibration error
   (ECE, 10 equal-count bins) and the Brier score over all held-out rows.
   * **2a:** VE's calibrated ECE is at most 0.05 and below its raw ECE.
   * **2b:** CLIP's calibrated ECE is at most 0.05 and below its raw ECE.
     Frames with no crop count as score 0.
   * Recorded, no bar: isotonic regression in place of Platt; ECE per
     held-out object.
3. **Today's thresholds, read through calibration** (recorded, no bar; this
   is the description the next decision starts from). For each held-out
   object:
   * the precision and recall of VE's `yes` at 0.5;
   * the share of in-view frames VE calls `no` at 0.1 (a missed target);
   * the share of frames in the unable band (0.1, 0.5).
4. **The router.** A logistic regression predicts "VE's decision is
   wrong", fitted held out. The decisions are VE's yes and no; unable
   answers already go to the cloud and are left out. Wrong means a yes
   with the target out of view, or a no with it in view. Its inputs are:
   * VE's score, and the box VE drew (its area share of the frame, or
     none);
   * CLIP's P and perception's status, and its best box's area share;
   * the disagreement: perception DETECTED and VE no, or perception
     absent and VE yes.
   * **4a:** AUROC at least 0.80 over all held-out rows.
   * **4b:** flagging at most 10% of VE's decided frames, it catches at
     least 50% of VE's wrong decisions.
   * **4c:** at the same flag rate it catches at least as many as the
     hand rule "flag when the two tiers disagree" (B4's novelty signal).
   * **If VE makes fewer than 20 wrong decisions in all, criterion 4 is
     recorded as not measurable, not met.**
5. **The gap, for the Astra decision.** Of the 535 in-view frames, the
   share where perception is not DETECTED (P < 0.8 or no crop), and of
   those, the share VE answers yes with a box.
   * **The rule, written now:** an Astra steering replay on the Rover's
     frames is worth costing (a separate yes, with a pilot) only if
     perception is silent on at least 20% of in-view frames AND VE's yes
     with a box covers less than half of that silent set. Otherwise the
     replay is not proposed.

**Out of scope.** Changing any threshold or the code: what this finds goes
to 3.91.C or to the behavior-tree section (point 5 of the 2026-10-22
discussion) as their input. Steering accuracy (3.80.I measured it on its
60 frames; this corpus labels presence, not direction).
