# 3.116 Room labels in a find mission, and an LLM's room prior

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.116; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-28, in a comment on the "A Thousand Houses"
page (Round 7): "Why not ask the LLM where the object might be in a house
and then use that as a fact in the RL. For instance a knife would be in
the kitchen". The reply: half of that is already built, and the half that
is missing is the room labels, not the prior. It proposed three steps; the
user answered "Yes" to writing them up as a plan section with bars set
before measuring. This is that section.

**What is known going in.**

* **The prior is already a fact.** Since 3.96, `RoomGoalPolicy`
  (`explore_goal_policy: rooms`, `brain/explore_goal_rooms.json`) gives
  every candidate `type_known`, `prior` and `prior_new` (prior x the room
  never entered). `prior[target][type]` is the share of the **training**
  houses' rooms of that type that hold at least one of the target's
  category: a per-room-type rate, **not** a distribution over types
  (basketball: bedroom 0.53, every other type 0; garbage can: bathroom
  0.90, kitchen 0.32, bedroom 0.20, living room 0.19). Four types only:
  ProcTHOR's kitchen, living room, bedroom, bathroom. A type the prior does
  not name reads as unknown (`goal_policy.py`).
* **The prior carries the saving, but only with labels.** 3.96's test
  houses (588; mode R = the target is one of the house's own objects):
  F-large R median goals 4 vs the rule's 7 with every room labelled, 5
  with 50% of labels dropped. On the selection houses with **every** label
  withheld (`wired/select.json`), R goals 7 = the rule's and metres +23%.
  Mode U (a backpack in a random room) keeps -20% goals without labels.
* **The labels in 3.96 came from a stand-in.** The simulator named the
  true room under a candidate once 25% of that room's floor was on the
  brain's map, **seen by the lidar**, whether or not the robot had entered
  it. A real label is a camera frame's answer about where the robot is
  standing (3.70 S2: `brain/room_labels.py`, `spacing` mode, an answer
  reaching at most one doorway on). So a real labeller names the rooms
  behind the robot well and the rooms ahead of it, where the prior matters,
  less well. **No measurement yet says how much of the saving survives
  that.**
* **Labels exist only in a survey today.** `room_label_source: cloud` (the
  vision service's `POST /room`, Opus 5.5, effort low, ~$0.006-0.009 a call,
  1.7-2.9 s) is wired for `job: survey` only. A find mission's map carries
  no `room_labels`. In the simulator `room_label_source: frame` reads the
  frame's own `room` (the truth at the robot).
* **Real labels are not perfect.** 3.70 S2 met the 95%-per-room bar on 46
  of 90 sim rooms (open plans, rooms never entered); real-frame accuracy
  is unmeasured (left for the Rover).
* **The gap that remains with perfect labels** (3.102 amendment 1) is
  mostly driving inside rooms, not finds. A room prior cannot close it.
  This section aims at keeping F-large's labelled saving on the car, not at
  the ceiling.

## Steps

**A. How much of the saving survives a real labeller (sim, no paid
calls).** In the 3.96 harness (`evaluations/goal-learn-396/`), F-large's
`best.json` unchanged, no retraining. Two new labeller arms beside 3.96's
stand-in and no-label rows:

* **A-ask:** labels only from asks, as `brain/room_labels.py` `spacing`
  would place them during the mission (every 2 m of walking, again through
  each doorway), each answer the true room **at the robot's position** (the
  sim's `frame` source), spread by the seeds' own reach rule. A candidate's
  type is the label on its cell (or on the cell beyond it for a frontier),
  else unknown.
* **A-ask-noisy:** A-ask with each answer replaced, with probability 0.15,
  by a different type drawn uniformly, seeded by house and ask. (0.15 is
  written now as a pessimistic stand-in for an unmeasured real error rate;
  it is not a measurement.)

Run on the 100 selection houses (`val` 100-199), modes R and U, then once
on the 588 test houses for the arm(s) that pass the gate. The rule,
stand-in and no-label rows are 3.96's, re-run on the same commit so every
row shares one code version. Ask counts per mission are recorded.

**Criteria for A (written now):**

1. **Recovery (the gate for B):** on the test houses, mode R, A-ask's
   median goals over found missions recover at least **half** of the
   stand-in's saving over the no-label arm: `goals(no-label) - goals(A-ask)
   >= 0.5 x (goals(no-label) - goals(stand-in))`, rounded as 3.96
   amendment 4 rounds. If A-ask does not, B does not start, and the
   section records what a better labeller would need (for example naming
   the room seen through a doorway, a different question from "where am
   I") and stops for the user.
2. **Finds:** A-ask and A-ask-noisy each find >= the rule's minus 1% in
   modes R and U.
3. **Noise does not turn the prior harmful:** A-ask-noisy's R median goals
   no more than the no-label arm's, and its R median metres no more than
   the no-label arm's + 10%.
4. **Asks are affordable:** A-ask's median asks per found mission <= 20
   and its 90th percentile <= 40 (at ~$0.009 a call, <= $0.18 median).
   Reported for both modes; judged in R.

**B. Room labels in a find mission (code, behind a flag).**
`brain.find_room_labels` (default `off`; `frame` | `cloud`): a find
mission runs the survey's labeller (`RoomLabeller`, the same `spacing`
mode, `room_label_max_calls` as its own cap `find_room_label_max_calls`,
default 20 from criterion 4) and feeds its layer to `RoomGoalPolicy` only
(never written into the map explore reads, so the rule's own room goals
stay off, as in 3.96). Asks go through the existing wall-clock bound and
the tick (3.70 S2's car half); a 401/403/404 stops asking for the mission,
as in a survey. Every ask is counted against the mission's paid calls.
Risky (paid cloud calls): `/code-review` and the Thermos pass.

**Criteria for B:**

5. **Same answers in process:** with `find_room_labels: frame` on the 100
   selection houses, the wired run reproduces A-ask's selection rows
   (outcome, goals, metres) in at least 98 of 100 houses per mode; every
   difference explained.
6. **Live:** three find missions against the local stack on
   `home_first_floor` with `find_room_labels: frame` and
   `explore_goal_policy: rooms`, through the brain's HTTP API: 3/3 `found`,
   0 contacts, asks within criterion 4's cap.
7. **Off is off:** with the flag off, 0 differences on the 100 selection
   houses against `dev` before the change.

**C. An LLM's room prior for any target.** One call per new target (cached
on disk by target text and model id) asks for the share of rooms of each
type that hold at least one such object, in the prior's own terms and
types (kitchen, living room, bedroom, bathroom), as numbers in [0, 1]. The
reply's shape is validated; a bad or missing reply leaves the target with
no prior (the policy's no-type path), never a guess. The model is the
vision service's default text-capable model at the time, confirmed by a
real call (CLAUDE.md section 6: never guess a model id). Spending: the
nine 3.96 categories plus about 20 everyday targets is ~30 calls, a few
cents; **asked before running.**

**Criteria for C (written now):**

8. **Agreement on the categories both know:** on 3.96's nine categories,
   the LLM's highest type matches the learned prior's highest type for
   every category whose learned top share is >= 0.6 (the sure ones:
   basketball, cart, garbage bag, plunger, garbage can), and the mean
   absolute difference over all 36 category-type cells is <= 0.20.
9. **No worse than the learned prior in the sim:** on the test houses,
   mode R, with the stand-in labeller, F-large reading the LLM's prior in
   place of the learned one: found >= the learned-prior arm's minus 1%,
   and median goals no more than the learned-prior arm's + 1.
10. **It answers for objects ProcTHOR lacks:** for the ~20 everyday
    targets (knife, keys, phone charger, toothbrush, remote control, ...;
    the list fixed in the harness before the first call), every reply
    parses and every value is in [0, 1]. (No accuracy bar: there is no
    ground truth for these in the sim. Their worth is measured in D.)

**D. On the Rover, in a real house (waits on the Rover and 3.70's real
room labels).** With `find_room_labels: cloud` and C's prior: real-frame
label accuracy per room, asks and dollars per mission, and goals and
metres against the rule on the same targets and starts. Bars are written
in an amendment before the first real run, once the Rover's walks give
the house's rooms and a target list.

**Amendment 1 (2026-10-28, before any A run): how A is built.**
`evaluations/goal-learn-396/ask_labels.py` `AskLabeller`, run by
`evaluations/goal-learn-396/ask.py` (`run OUT select|test`, `score OUT`);
`train._mission` takes a `labels` factory and calls its `step(runner)`
after every tick, where MissionRunner observes a survey's labeller.

* The asks are `RoomLabeller`'s own (`spacing`, 2 m, `DOOR_M` 3 m, reach
  `REACH_M`), uncapped so criterion 4 measures the count; the sim clock
  drives its re-cut. The answer is the true room at the robot's cell.
* A candidate's type is read off `RoomLabeller.layer()` on the map at the
  choice: the goal's own cell, else its 3x3 neighbours (the stand-in's own
  fallback, in place of "the cell beyond it" above). An answer reaching
  past a doorway names the room beyond with the asking room's name, as the
  real labeller would.
* Noise: each answer, with probability 0.15, becomes one of the other three
  types, uniformly; one seeded stream per house.
* The full run is the 588 test houses, every arm, once; criteria 1-4 are
  judged there. The 100 selection houses are a smoke check only.

### Order and dependencies

A first (no spend, no code in `brain/`). B only if criterion 1 is met. C
can run beside B (it needs only the harness and a few cents, asked first).
D after the Rover arrives. If A fails criterion 1, B and D wait on a
labeller that names rooms ahead (a section of its own), and C is still
worth running as a record.
