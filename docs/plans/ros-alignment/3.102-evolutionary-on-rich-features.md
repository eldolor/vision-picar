# 3.102 The evolutionary method on 3.96's and 3.101's fact sets: E-small, E-large, E-rich against their gradient twins

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.102; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-10-25: "Did we also re-run the evolutionary model
with F-small, F-large, F-rich parameter sets to clearly compare each set's
results against the gradient method?" -- then "add it to the plan and run
all three". No: the evolutionary method (3.65's cross-entropy method, CEM)
has only ever run on the original ten facts (3.65; 3.100 on 613 fresh
houses, where it tied the gradient network at -11%). F-small, F-large and
F-rich (3.96, 3.101) were all trained the gradient way only (clone the
oracle, then PPO). So with 17 or 42 facts it is unknown how much of their
gain is the facts and how much the method.

**The arms.** Each a weighted sum of its facts, no network (3.96's F
network with its MLP zeroed, so `mission()` and `score_np` run it as they
are):

| Arm | Facts | Training houses | Gradient twin |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-small | 17 (3.96's) | 570 (3.96 A's set) | F-small |
| E-large | 17 | 5,464 (3.96's large set) | F-large |
| E-rich | 42 (3.101's) | 5,464 | F-rich |

**Training, the same for every arm** (`evaluations/goal-learn-3102/cem.py`):
3.65's loop and settings -- 24 guesses from N(mean, std), the best 6 by mean
reward refit the mean and std, 40 houses a generation, std from 2.0 with a
floor of 0.2 -- for **24 generations, 23,040 training searches**, the
gradient arms' count (60 x 48 x 8). The start is 3.65's rule-like weights
on the ten old facts and 0 on every newer fact, so nothing is borrowed from
the gradient runs. All guesses of a generation play the same houses in the
same target modes (R or U, half and half). Every 4 generations (and at the
start) the mean plays the 100 selection houses in both modes; the best
mode-R check is kept, as 3.96 kept its arms' best. Seed 3102. Records
only: nothing in `brain/` changes.

**Test, once, every E arm** (the point is the comparison, so there is no
gate): the 588 test houses, modes R and U. The rule's, F-large's and
F-rich's rows are 3.101's (2026-10-25), which equal 3.96 C's records
exactly. F-small was never run on the test houses, so E-small meets its twin
on the selection houses only.

**Criteria (written before any run; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **The method question (E-large vs F-large, E-rich vs F-rich, mode R):**
   a paired bootstrap over the test houses (10,000 draws, seed 3102) of the
   mean reward difference. The 95% interval decides: **level** if it holds
   0, **better** or **worse** if it lies wholly above or below. The
   evolutionary method "keeps up" at a fact set if it is level or better.
   Mode U the same, reported.
2. **The 20% goal for each E arm** (3.96's criterion 2, unchanged): mode R
   found >= 99% of the rule's and median goals, or median metres, >= 20%
   below the rule's; mode U found >= 99% of the rule's and median goals no
   more than the rule's.
3. **Clean:** 100% of each E arm's test missions end with an outcome, no
   exception, under the tick cap (reported per arm).

Reported, not judged: the selection curves beside the twins'; median goals
and metres; houses where each arm takes the shorter search; the learned
weights (which facts the linear model leans on).

**Where it runs.** This cloud container (4 cores), no spend.

**Amendment 1 (2026-10-25, the user, during training and before any test
run): the oracle's ceiling on the test houses.** F-large and F-rich came out
level, and the user asked whether the network's architecture is the next
lever. Before building one, measure how much there is left to win, and
where. **The oracle** (3.88 B's: it picks the goal on the true shortest
floor path to the target; explore still has to see and reach it) runs on
the same 588 test houses, modes R and U, once, after the E arms, by the same
harness (`cem.py test`, arm `oracle`).

* **Reported:** the oracle's found, median goals and metres, and mean
  reward. Every arm's mean-reward gap to it (rule, F-large, F-rich and the
  three E arms) split the way the reward is built: **finds** (+1 a find),
  **goals** (-0.02 each) and **metres** (-0.01 each), per mode.
* **Decision rule, written now:** for F-rich in mode R, if the finds part
  of its gap is at least the goals and metres parts together, the next work
  is explore's misses (the exhaustions and livelocks 3.96 classified), not
  the chooser. Otherwise a chooser that scores the whole pool together
  (attention over the candidates) earns its own section. Either way the
  user decides.
* The oracle is a ceiling, not a candidate: it reads the target's position,
  which the robot never has.

**Read with care:**

* **E-rich's selection score did not carry to the test:** 0.587 on the
  100 selection houses, 0.434 on the 588 test houses, below the rule
  (0.450). Its best was one of seven noisy checks; the gradient arms'
  selection-to-test drop was far smaller (F-rich 0.644 -> 0.563).
* **"Method" here is the whole recipe,** not the learner alone. The E arms
  are weighted sums with no hidden layers and learn only from search
  rewards; the F arms add hidden layers and first copy the oracle's
  choices, then refine by PPO. At ten facts (3.100) the two recipes tied;
  with 17 and 42 facts the gradient recipe pulls ahead. Which part of it
  matters (the oracle copy, the hidden layers, or credit per choice) is
  not separated here.
* **More houses did not help breeding** (E-small 0.421, E-large 0.406 in
  R): with 24 guesses a generation, 17 weights are already more than random
  tweaks search well. The extra facts did help it (E-rich best in U at
  0.420, 22% fewer goals than the rule there).
* E-rich's weights are mostly its rule-like start (goal type -11.6, path
  -9.8) with noise-sized pulls on the new facts, some with the wrong sign
  (unknown space near the goal -5.6): little to read into them.

**Amendment 1, the oracle's ceiling.** The oracle finds 574 of 583 (R) and
583 of 588 (U), with a median of 2 and 3 goals and 8.4 and 10.5 m: mean
reward 0.783 and 0.781. Every arm's gap to it, split as the reward is
built:

| Gap to the oracle, mode R | Finds | Goals | Metres | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rule | 0.007 | 0.125 | 0.201 | 0.333 |
| F-large | 0.009 | 0.066 | 0.155 | 0.230 |
| **F-rich** | **0.012** | **0.061** | **0.147** | **0.220** |
| E-small / E-large / E-rich | 0.004 / 0.014 / 0.009 | 0.139 / 0.146 / 0.116 | 0.220 / 0.218 / 0.224 | 0.362 / 0.377 / 0.349 |

Mode U is the same shape (F-rich: finds -0.003, goals 0.091, metres 0.246).
**By the rule written before the run: F-rich's finds part (0.012) is far
below its goals and metres parts (0.208), so the chooser has room, and
3.103 step B (lean facts, a shape sweep) goes ahead** -- the user decides.
The room is mostly **metres**: the oracle drives about 40% less than F-rich
at the median. An earlier estimate in this session ("about one trip of
headroom", from 3.88's oracle on other houses) was wrong; on these houses
the gap is about two trips and 6 m at the median.

**What it means.** The gradient recipe (copy the oracle, then PPO, with a
small network) is the better learner here, by a clear margin once the facts
grow past ten; the evolutionary method should not be pursued further on
this decision. And the learned choosers are far from done: the oracle shows
another 0.22 of reward on the table, almost all of it in driving less,
which is a chooser problem, not explore's misses. Records only; nothing in
`brain/` changes.
