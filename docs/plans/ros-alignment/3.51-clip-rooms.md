# 3.51 Rooms from CLIP: an early read on the four rig walks

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.51; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** On real frames the room the robot is in comes only from the cloud:
`brain/agent.py` backfills `frame["room"]` from `/navigate`'s `room_guess`
(`brain/rooms.py`'s `identify_room()` is a sim-era keyword matcher that no
real path calls). The tiered policy already keeps CLIP RN50 resident for
target matching, so scoring a whole frame against a handful of room prompts
might give a room label on the robot, every frame, with no cloud call.
Idea 2 of the 2026-10-07 comparison with XPENG/Banma's "on-device first"
cockpit; approved by the user 2026-10-09, who chose to measure on the
**existing walks only**.

**What this can and cannot say.** The four adjudicated rig walks of
2026-09-07 (299 frames) were recorded in two or three rooms of one house,
mostly the living room. This section gives an early read: whether CLIP
separates the rooms these walks cover, at what cost. **It cannot settle
whether CLIP recognises rooms in general**, and its numbers must not be
quoted as if it did.

**Measurement only.** Nothing is wired into the mission path.

**Frame set (pinned).** `blue-bottle-20260907-142454` (33),
`red-backpack-20260907-144856` (38), `blue-shoes-20260907-152528` (19),
`blue-bottle-20260907-185007` (209): 299 frames, from the local copy under
`recordings/`. S3 is not written.

**Reference.** A per-frame room label made by looking at each frame
(contact sheets of the frames, in sequence order, so a frame's neighbours
are visible), stored as a new sidecar `room_labels.json` beside each walk's
local copy. `labels.json` is not modified. A frame whose room cannot be
told from the picture is `unsure` and is excluded from every accuracy
number (its count is reported). The cloud's recorded `room_guess`
(`walk.jsonl`, Opus 4.5) is evidence about the model, never the reference.
Frames where my label and the cloud's disagree are listed for the user to
settle (the project's rule: the user adjudicates disagreements only); the
numbers below use my labels until then.

**Method, fixed before measuring.**
* Classes: a closed set of seven room types fixed before looking at any
  CLIP output, regardless of which rooms the walks contain, so CLIP has to
  pick against realistic alternatives: living room, kitchen, bedroom,
  hallway, bathroom, dining room, home office.
* Prompts: three templates, `"a photo of a {room}."`,
  `"a photo of a {room}, taken from the floor."`, `"the inside of a
  {room}."`. The primary score is the mean of the three normalised text
  embeddings per class (prompt ensembling); each template is also reported
  alone.
* Image: the whole frame through `ClipScorer`'s own preprocess (RN50,
  `openai` weights), argmax over classes.
* Tuning: if anything is chosen from results (a template, N for
  smoothing), it is chosen on the three short walks only and reported on
  the held-out 209-frame walk, which is also the only one that crosses rooms.

**Criteria (written before measuring; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **Frame accuracy** (primary prompts, per frame, vs my labels, unsure
   excluded): >= 80% over all 299 frames' decided labels.
2. **Macro accuracy:** mean per-room recall over every labelled room with
   >= 10 frames: >= 70%. (A model that answers "living room" to everything
   would pass criterion 1 on this corpus; this is the guard.)
3. **Against the cloud:** CLIP's frame accuracy is within 5 points of the
   cloud's recorded `room_guess` accuracy on the same frames (room names
   normalised; "unclear" counted as wrong). Agreement between the two is
   reported beside it.
4. **Added cost:** one whole-frame image encode per frame, on top of
   perception (`perceive.py` encodes only crops; there is no whole-frame
   embedding to reuse). Laptop only, CPU and MPS reported separately, never
   merged with GPU rows (P24). Bar: laptop-CPU p90 <= 62 ms (a quarter of
   the 250 ms perception budget, on hardware slower than the Jetson's GPU).
   The Jetson is not used here: its number is recorded as not measured.
5. **Stability, paired with progress (criterion 1):** a majority vote over
   the last N frames (N chosen on the short walks from 3, 5, 7) cuts room
   switches per 100 frames on the held-out walk by >= 50% while losing at
   most 2 points of frame accuracy there.

**Labels made 2026-10-09** (`docs/evaluations/room-labels-3.51.json`; a
`room_labels.json` beside each walk's local copy): 299 frames, **224
decided** -- living room 154, hallway 60 (the foyer / stair hall), home
office 10 -- and 75 `unsure` (no room cue in the frame, or the camera in a
doorway: frames 0120-0125 and 0186-0201 of the long walk). The three short
walks are all the living room; only the 209-frame walk crosses rooms
(living room -> stair hall -> home office). So: three rooms, one house, and
one of them on 10 frames.

**Measured 2026-10-09** (`python -m tools.clip_rooms_eval score|report`;
per-frame results in `docs/evaluations/clip-rooms-3.51-cpu.json` and
`-mps.json`, pinned by `tests/test_clip_rooms_eval.py`). Laptop CPU and MPS
predict the same room on all 299 frames under every prompt; only the
timing differs. Timings use the repo's one percentile definition
(`brain/tiered.py` `percentiles()`, integer ms), after 3 synchronised
warm-up encodes.

Each template alone: `"a photo of a {room}."` 64.7%, `"... taken from the
floor."` 67.0%, `"the inside of a {room}."` 70.5% -- none reaches the bar,
and picking the best after the fact would be tuning on the test set.
Held-out walk alone (the only multi-room one): 56.3%, macro 38.5%.

**Where it goes wrong.** Of 60 stair-hall frames CLIP calls 27 living room
and 14 home office; of 10 office frames it gets 1. It is good at the room
it sees most (living room, 137 / 154), which is what criterion 2 was
written to catch. The cloud is also weak off the living room (hallway 16 /
60, office 4 / 10), so a floor-height photo of a hallway is hard for both.

**Frames for the user to settle** (my label vs the cloud's recorded
`room_guess`, all on `blue-bottle-20260907-185007`; 51 of the 224):

* 0126-0181 (43 frames: 126-131, 133-136, 138, 139, 141, 142, 144, 145,
  147-152, 154-167, 169, 171, 173-176, 181): I say **hallway** (stair hall
  with the grey cabinet and the vacuum); the cloud says living room. If the
  stair hall counts as part of the living room, CLIP's accuracy becomes
  165 / 224 = 73.7%: **criterion 1 still fails.**
* 0202, 0203, 0205, 0211, 0212: I say home office (desk, screen); the
  cloud says living room. 0209: the cloud says hallway.
* 0185: I say hallway; the cloud says bedroom. 0118: I say living room
  (sofa in view, looking out); the cloud says hallway.

**Verdict on this corpus.** Off-the-shelf CLIP RN50 with plain room prompts
does not recognise rooms well enough to replace the cloud's room guess:
criteria 1-4 failed, 5 met. That is an early read on three rooms of one
house at floor height, not a general result. It says plain zero-shot
prompting is not enough; it does not test a few-shot approach (embeddings
of labelled frames from the user's own rooms as prototypes), which is the
cheaper next thing to try and needs the walk set below.

**What would settle it.** A floor-height walk through every room of the
house (kitchen, bedrooms, bathroom, dining room included), 30+ decided
frames per room, labelled the same way. The local corpus already has 17
more labelled walks (2026-09-08 to 09-21) that were not in this frame set;
their rooms were not checked here and may cover more of the house.
